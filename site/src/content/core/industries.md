## Industries Hub – the short answer

Dazzlit works with 17 industries, from healthcare and law firms to SaaS, home services, retail and nonprofits. Each industry gets a playbook built around its buyers, its rules and its seasons, run by specialists who know the sector and its customers.

## Why it matters / context

Marketing that works in one industry often fails in another. A law firm and a home services company both need leads, but their buyers search differently, decide differently and trust different signals. A SaaS buyer reads comparison pages and wants a trial; a dental patient wants a practice nearby with good reviews; a manufacturer wants a supplier who understands the specification.

That is why Dazzlit plans by industry as well as by channel. The buyer personas, the customer journey and the marketing mix are built for the sector first, and the channels — search, paid, social, content — are chosen to fit. The KPIs change too: a franchise needs cost per location lead, a B2B technology firm needs pipeline, a nonprofit needs donations and volunteers.

## Key factors & examples

The starting point is always the buyer. In [healthcare](/industries/healthcare/) and [dental](/industries/dental/), trust and local visibility decide most choices, so search, reviews and clear service pages matter most. In [law firms](/industries/law-firms/), the enquiry is high value and the decision is careful, so content that answers real questions and a website that converts matter more than volume.

In [SaaS](/industries/saas/) and [B2B technology](/industries/technology/), the journey is longer and involves several decision makers, so the funnel needs content for each stage, from comparison to trial, and attribution that follows the deal rather than the click. For [startups](/industries/startups/), speed matters: paid media and content that test messages fast, then search that compounds the winners.

Local and seasonal businesses — [home services](/industries/home-services/), [real estate](/industries/real-estate/), [restaurants and hospitality](/industries/restaurants-hospitality/), [automotive](/industries/automotive/), [retail](/industries/retail/) and [franchise](/industries/franchise/) — live or die on local search, reviews and timely offers, and their brand positioning has to survive in a feed full of competitors. Regulated and specialist sectors — [finance](/industries/finance/), [manufacturing](/industries/manufacturing/), [education](/industries/education/), [events](/industries/events/) and [nonprofit](/industries/nonprofit/) — need claims that are accurate, language their audience trusts, and measurement that respects first-party data. Across all of them, ROI, ROAS, customer acquisition cost and lifetime value are reported the same way, so results can be compared honestly.

## Mistakes to avoid

The first mistake is copying a playbook from another industry. A tactic that works for ecommerce rarely works for a law firm, and a SaaS funnel rarely fits a dental practice. If an agency shows you results from a different sector and calls them yours, ask what they will do differently for your buyers.

The second is ignoring the rules of the sector. Healthcare, finance and legal marketing all have claims you cannot make and words you cannot use. Get the compliance right before the creative, not after.

The third is measuring the wrong thing. Awareness metrics flatter a campaign that produces no enquiries. Agree the KPIs that match your business — leads, sales, cost per acquisition — before the work starts.
