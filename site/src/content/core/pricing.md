## Packages by service & tier

Dazzlit builds every package around the services you actually need, not a fixed bundle. A package can cover one channel, such as [SEO services](/services/seo/) or [PPC](/services/ppc-management/), or combine several — search, paid media, [social media management](/services/social-media-marketing/) and [content marketing](/services/content-marketing/) — into one plan with one team and one report.

Packages are organised by scope. A single-channel package suits a business that needs one thing done well: technical SEO fixes, a Google Ads account rebuilt, or a content programme that supports the rest of the marketing mix. A multi-channel package suits a business that needs several channels pulling in the same direction, with shared buyer personas, one customer journey mapped across touchpoints, and one set of KPIs so every channel is judged on the same outcomes.

The fullest scope is omnichannel: search, paid, social and content planned together, so short-term sales and long-term brand growth support each other instead of competing for budget. Scope also grows with markets. A package that covers one country is a different job from one that covers the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia, with local search behaviour, language and regulation in each.

Whatever the scope, every package names the services, the markets, the deliverables and the reporting rhythm up front. The proposal you receive lists what is included, what is not, and what success looks like, so you can compare it with any other quote on the same terms.

A package is never set and forgotten. It starts with an audit of your channels, your audience and your competitors, so the scope is based on evidence rather than guesswork. Once work begins, the scope is reviewed every month against the numbers: channels that earn their budget keep it, channels that do not are changed or paused, and new opportunities — a market that opens up, a format that works — are folded in when the data supports them.

## What affects price

Four things decide what a package costs: scope, markets, competition and speed.

Scope is the biggest factor. More channels, more content and more markets mean more specialist time. A package that runs search and paid media in one market is a smaller engagement than one that runs five channels across three markets with content in two languages. The marketing funnel matters too: work that covers the whole funnel, from awareness to repeat purchase, costs more than work that covers one stage, because it needs more creative, more landing pages and more measurement.

Competition sets the effort required. In a crowded market, ranking on Google or winning an auction on Google Ads takes more work, more testing and more content than in a quiet niche. Your starting point matters as well: a site with a clean technical base and existing authority moves faster than one that needs fixing first.

Speed is the last factor. A launch with a fixed date — a product release, a seasonal peak, an event — needs more people working in parallel, and that shows in the fee. A steady programme with no deadline can be phased, which spreads the work and the cost.

Two things that do not change the fee are the reporting and the measurement. Every package includes monthly reporting on leads, sales, revenue and cost per acquisition, with GA4 and attribution set up so the numbers are trusted, and first-party data used where third-party data falls short.

Media spend is separate from the agency fee. Money paid to Google, Meta, TikTok or any other platform goes to the platform, not to Dazzlit, and it is reported separately so you can always see what went to media and what went to the work. The fee covers the specialists, the creative, the content, the testing and the reporting; the media budget is yours, it stays under your accounts, and it can be raised or lowered as results dictate.

## Retainer vs project vs performance

Most clients work with Dazzlit on a retainer: a fixed monthly scope that runs until the results say otherwise. A retainer suits ongoing growth — SEO that compounds month after month, paid media that is tested and improved every week, and content that builds authority over time. The scope is agreed in advance, reviewed monthly, and changed when the data says something else deserves the budget. It is the right model when the goal is steady growth in leads and revenue rather than a single launch.

A project suits a defined piece of work with a beginning and an end. A website rebuild, a one-off audit, a market-entry plan or a campaign for a single event are all projects: the deliverables are listed, the timeline is fixed, and the fee is agreed before work starts. Projects work well when the scope is clear and the outcome is a finished asset rather than an ongoing result.

Performance pricing ties part of the fee to results, such as leads generated or revenue attributed to the work. It suits businesses with clean measurement — reliable attribution, a clear value per lead, and agreement on what counts as a result — and it is usually combined with a retainer rather than replacing it, because the work still needs to be paid for while results build. Which model fits depends on your goals, your measurement and how much risk you want to share.
