## Why it matters / context

A proposal is not a sales call. It is the first piece of real work: a senior strategist looks at your business, your market and your competition, and writes down what they would do and why. That is the document you judge Dazzlit on, before any money changes hands. It is free, itemised and without obligation, and it is the same whether you come through the form, by email or by booking a call.

The form below asks for three things: the services you need, the market you sell in, and your budget range, plus your name, work email and website. That is enough to write a useful plan. If you already know more — the channels you have tried, the numbers you are getting, the date you need results by — add it in your message and it will be used.

## Mistakes to avoid

The first mistake is sending a vague brief. "We need marketing" gives a strategist nothing to plan against, and the proposal that comes back will be generic. A few lines about your goal, your buyers and your budget will get you a plan worth reading.

The second is hiding the budget. A budget is not a commitment; it is the information that lets a proposal fit the money available. Without it, the plan may be too big, too small, or built for the wrong channels entirely.

The third is asking for a price without a plan. The fee depends on scope, markets, competition and speed, and a number without that context means little. Judge the proposal on what it includes and what it will be measured on — leads, sales, revenue and cost per acquisition — rather than on the fee alone.
