## Case Studies – the short answer

Dazzlit case studies show real work with real numbers: an SEO programme that grew organic traffic by 215% in 6 months and multiplied leads 3.2 times, Google Ads that generated 450+ qualified leads at 38% lower cost per lead, and a website redesign that raised conversions by 160%.

## Why it matters / context

Any agency can say it gets results. A case study is the proof: what the business needed, what was done, and what changed, with numbers that can be checked against the work. It is also the fastest way to judge whether an agency can help your business, because the shape of the problem matters more than the size of the claim.

Read a case study for the fit, not the headline. A 215% traffic increase means little on its own; what matters is whether the leads that followed were the right ones, whether the cost per lead fell, and whether the work would survive in your market, with your buyers and your competition. The three studies here are different for that reason: one is about search, one about paid media and one about the website itself.

## Key factors & examples

The SEO study is about a local service business that needed more organic leads. The work was to improve Google rankings, attract qualified visitors and turn that traffic into enquiries. Organic traffic rose 215% in 6 months, and leads multiplied 3.2 times. The lesson is that search works when the whole customer journey is fixed — rankings, content and the pages that convert — not when a single ranking is chased.

The paid advertising study is about a home services company that needed more customers at a lower cost. Targeted Google Ads campaigns were built and optimised to bring in better enquiries while cutting waste. The result was 450+ qualified leads with cost per lead down 38%. The lesson is that paid media is a testing discipline: the campaigns that win are the ones that are measured on cost per acquisition and improved every week.

The web design study is about a business website that was outdated and losing visitors. The redesign rebuilt the experience around the buyer: modern design, clear messaging and landing pages built to convert. Conversions rose 160% and engagement rose 72%. The lesson is that the website is the end of every funnel — search, ads and social all send people there — so fixing it lifts everything else. The same funnel thinking runs through [our services](/services/): the channels bring buyers in, and the website turns them into customers.

## Mistakes to avoid

The first mistake is judging a case study by one number. A traffic increase without leads, or a lead increase without sales, is not a result. Ask what happened further down the funnel: enquiries, cost per acquisition, revenue and lifetime value.

The second is assuming a result transfers. A tactic that worked for a home services company in one market may not work for a SaaS business in another. Ask what was specific to that client — its buyers, its competition, its starting point — and what would be different for you.

The third is ignoring the work behind the number. Results come from a plan that covers positioning, buyer personas, the marketing mix and measurement, with attribution and GA4 set up so the numbers are trusted. If a study cannot explain what was done, the number is not worth much.
