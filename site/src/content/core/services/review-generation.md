## Review Generation & Management Services that drive measurable growth

Reviews are the first thing a buyer checks and the last thing they remember. Before calling, booking or buying, people read what other customers said — the star rating, the recent reviews, and how the business answered the unhappy ones. Review generation is the work of earning more of those reviews, honestly and steadily; review management is the work of answering them, learning from them, and making sure they show up where buyers look.

Dazzlit is a review generation and management agency that runs the whole loop. We ask happy customers for reviews at the right moment, make it easy with a single link, answer every review in your voice, and report on the star rating, the volume and the sentiment across Google Business Profile, Trustpilot, Yelp, G2 and the review sites that matter in your industry. The reviews we earn are real, from real customers, with no incentives and no fake reviews — because review policies forbid them, and because a rating built on fake reviews collapses the moment a buyer reads one closely.

The work matters for two reasons. First, buyers trust reviews more than advertising, so more genuine reviews mean more enquiries and more sales. Second, reviews feed search: a business with a steady flow of recent, positive Google reviews ranks better in local search and wins more clicks from the map. Review velocity — how many reviews you earn each month — matters as much as the average rating, and both are measured and reported.

Reviews also shape what search engines show. A business with a steady flow of recent, positive Google reviews ranks better in local search, and review schema can put star ratings directly into search results, which wins more clicks from the same position. That is why velocity matters: a burst of reviews once a year does less than a steady few each week. We track velocity alongside volume and rating, so a quiet month is noticed and dealt with instead of discovered a quarter later.

### Review requests, responses and reporting

Underneath the named services, the everyday work is three habits done well. Review requests go out at the right moment, by email or SMS, with a single link and no friction. Review responses go out fast — thanking happy customers in a way that sounds like you, and answering unhappy ones with an apology, a fix and an invitation to talk, not an argument. And reporting ties it together: review volume, review velocity, average star rating, response rate and response time, and sentiment across every site. Testimonials and user-generated content grow out of the same work: with permission, the best reviews become the words on your website, in your ads and on your social channels. Our guide to [user generated content](/resources/guides/user-generated-content/) explains how to use customer words well.

Answering an unhappy customer well is often worth more than the review itself. A calm, specific reply — an apology, what you are doing about it, and an invitation to talk — is read by every future buyer who opens that review, and it shows the business listens. We also watch for patterns: if several reviews mention the same delivery problem or the same confusing instruction, that is not a review problem, it is a business problem, and we flag it so it can be fixed at the source.

## Our review generation process (audit → strategy → execution → reporting)

Every engagement follows the same four stages, so you always know where the work stands and what happens next.

**Audit.** We start by looking at your reviews as a buyer would: your Google Business Profile and the review sites your customers use, your star rating and review volume, how fast you answer, and what customers actually complain about. We check review velocity — how many reviews you earn each month — and compare it with competitors, and we look at whether review schema is set up so your stars show in search results. The audit ends with a written picture of your reviews as they stand, and a list of what to fix first.

**Strategy.** Next we agree the plan: which review sites matter most, when and how to ask, who answers and in what voice, and what to do with unhappy customers. A business with few reviews gets a plan weighted toward requests; a business with plenty of reviews but slow answers gets one weighted toward responses and sentiment. The strategy names the numbers we will be judged on — review volume, star rating, response rate and sentiment — so there is no argument later about what success looks like.

**Execution.** Then we do the work: review links and QR codes set up, requests running by email or SMS, responses written in your voice, unhappy customers handled with care, and the best reviews turned into testimonials and user-generated content. Each part is run by its specialist, but the plan is shared, so a response on Google matches the tone of a reply on Trustpilot and the facts on your website. Where reviews sit inside a bigger picture, our [online reputation management](/services/reputation-management/) service and our [local SEO services](/services/local-seo/) team work alongside.

**Reporting.** Every month you get one report across all review sites, covering review volume, velocity, average star rating, response rate and response time, and sentiment. We explain what changed, why it changed and what we are doing next. If a location is falling behind, we say so and fix it.
