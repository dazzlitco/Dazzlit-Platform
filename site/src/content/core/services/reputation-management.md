## Online Reputation Management Services that drive measurable growth

Your reputation is what people find when they search for you — and most of them search before they call, book or buy. Online reputation management is the work of shaping what that search shows: the reviews, the articles, the social media posts, the knowledge panel and the search results that together form a first impression. When the picture is good, it brings in customers. When it is not, it quietly turns them away.

Dazzlit is an online reputation management agency that manages the whole picture, not just one part of it. We monitor review sites and social media, respond to reviews, build the positive content that earns its place in search, and work to suppress negative content by giving search engines better, more relevant pages to show. Where content breaks a platform's rules — fake reviews, for example — we help you dispute it through the proper channels. What we do not do is invent reviews or make promises about deleting content we do not control; reputation work that lasts is built on real reviews, real content and real service.

The work covers three things that matter to a buyer. First, the star rating and the words next to it on Google and the review sites. Second, the search results for your name — your website, your profiles, your press and any negative pages that sit too high. Third, what people say about you on social media, and how quickly and well you answer. Each one is watched, measured and reported on, so you always know where your reputation stands.

Two parts of the picture deserve special attention. The knowledge panel — the box of facts Google shows beside search results — is often the first thing a searcher reads, so we make sure the information in it is accurate and complete. And for people as well as brands, executive reputation matters: a founder or chief executive is searched by investors, partners and journalists, and a thin or negative first page for their name reflects on the whole business. Both are built the same way as everything else in reputation work: accurate information, real content, and steady attention over time.

## Our reputation management process (audit → strategy → execution → reporting)

Every engagement follows the same four stages, so you always know where the work stands and what happens next.

**Audit.** We start by searching for you the way a customer would: your name and your brand on Google, on the review sites, and on social media. We record your star rating and review volume, list the pages that rank for your name, check your knowledge panel and profiles, and measure sentiment across mentions. The audit ends with a written picture of your reputation as it stands — the good, the bad and the gaps — and a list of what to fix first.

**Strategy.** Next we agree the plan: what to monitor, which review sites matter most, what content to build, and how to handle negative reviews and crises. A business with a few bad reviews gets a plan weighted toward review generation and responses; a brand with a damaging article ranking on page one gets one weighted toward content and search results management. The strategy names the numbers we will be judged on — star rating, review volume, sentiment and first-page results — so there is no argument later about what success looks like.

**Execution.** Then we do the work: monitoring set up, review requests running, responses written in your voice, content published, profiles fixed, disputes filed where the rules allow. Each part is run by its specialist, but the plan is shared, so a response on Google matches the tone of a reply on social media and the facts on your website.

**Reporting.** Every month you get one report across all of it, covering star rating, review volume and sentiment, search results for your name, and mentions across review sites and social media. We explain what changed, why it changed and what we are doing next. Reputation moves slowly, and the report shows the movement — good or bad — honestly.

## Reputation Management pricing & packages

There is no single price for reputation management, because no two reputations need the same work. What affects price is straightforward: how much there is to monitor, how many review sites and search results are involved, whether you need ongoing management or help with a specific problem, and how many locations or people are covered. A small business with a few listings costs less to manage than a brand with a large search footprint.

We work in three engagement models. A monthly retainer covers the ongoing monitoring, responses and reporting, with the team sized to the plan. A project covers a defined piece of work, such as an audit, a crisis response or a content programme. A performance arrangement ties part of our fee to the results we agree in advance. Most clients start on a retainer and add projects as they grow. Your proposal will set out the scope, the work and the price in writing before anything starts.
