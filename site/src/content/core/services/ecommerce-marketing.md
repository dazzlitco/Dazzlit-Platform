## eCommerce Marketing Services that drive measurable growth

An online store competes everywhere at once. Shoppers search on Google, compare prices on marketplaces, scroll past your products on social media, and decide in seconds whether to buy from you or from the store next door. eCommerce marketing is the work of showing up in all of those places with one clear message, and turning the visits that follow into orders, repeat purchases and revenue you can measure.

Dazzlit is an eCommerce marketing agency that plans search, paid media, email, SMS, content and marketplaces as one plan. Instead of running each channel on its own, we start from your numbers — average order value, conversion rate, customer acquisition cost and customer lifetime value — and build the mix of channels that moves them. A store that needs new customers gets a different plan from a store that needs more from the customers it already has, and a direct-to-consumer (DTC) brand launching in a new market gets a different plan again.

That focus on the numbers is what makes the work measurable. Every channel has a job: paid search and shopping bring in buyers who are ready to purchase, email and SMS bring back the shoppers who left without buying, content answers the questions that stand between a visitor and a checkout, and reviews and user-generated content give new buyers a reason to trust you. When the channels share one strategy and one report, you can see which ones earn their keep and which ones need fixing.

We work with stores on Shopify and other platforms, in the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, the UAE and Singapore. Whether you sell ten products or ten thousand, the approach is the same: find the buyers, remove the friction, and keep them coming back.

Retention is where the profit sits. Winning a first order costs far more than winning a second one, so the plan always includes the work that brings customers back: loyalty programs that reward repeat buying, post-purchase emails that suggest the next product, and win-back flows for customers who have gone quiet. We measure retention alongside acquisition — repeat purchase rate, time between orders and lifetime value — because a store that keeps its customers can afford to spend more to win new ones. That is also why attribution matters: when a shopper clicks an ad, reads a guide, opens an email and then buys, every channel in that journey deserves its share of the credit, and the report shows it that way.

Combined with reviews and user-generated content, loyalty turns one-time buyers into the customers who fund the next round of growth.

## Our eCommerce marketing process (audit → strategy → execution → reporting)

Every engagement follows the same four stages, so you always know where the work stands and what happens next.

**Audit.** We start by looking at the store as a buyer would: analytics, product feeds, checkout, email and SMS flows, ad accounts, search visibility and reviews. We find where shoppers drop off, which channels already earn their keep, and what the numbers say about average order value, retention and customer acquisition cost. The audit ends with a written list of what to fix first, in order of impact.

**Strategy.** Next we agree the plan: the channels, the markets, the budget and the targets. A store chasing first orders gets a plan weighted toward paid search and shopping; a store with a strong customer base gets one weighted toward email, SMS and loyalty programs. The strategy names the numbers we will be judged on — revenue, orders, acquisition cost and lifetime value — so there is no argument later about what success looks like.

**Execution.** Then we build and launch: feeds cleaned and structured, campaigns set up, flows written and tested, content published, listings improved. Each channel is run by its specialist, but the plan is shared, so a promotion in email matches the one in paid social and the stock levels on the site.

**Reporting.** Every month you get one report across all channels, covering revenue, orders, average order value, cost per acquisition and return on ad spend. We explain what changed, why it changed and what we are doing next. If a channel is not earning its keep, we say so and fix it or move the budget.

## eCommerce Marketing pricing & packages

There is no single price for eCommerce marketing, because no two stores need the same work. What affects price is straightforward: how many channels you need, the size of your product catalogue, how many markets you sell in, and how much media spend the plan requires. A store starting with email and paid search costs less to run than a store running paid media, content, marketplaces and SMS across six markets.

We work in three engagement models. A monthly retainer covers the ongoing running of your channels, with the team sized to the plan. A project covers a defined piece of work, such as a feed rebuild, a migration or a launch campaign. A performance arrangement ties part of our fee to the results we agree in advance. Most clients start on a retainer and add projects as they grow. For a closer look at what goes into pricing, read our guide to [eCommerce marketing pricing](/blog/ecommerce-marketing-pricing/). Your proposal will set out the channels, the work and the price in writing before anything starts.
