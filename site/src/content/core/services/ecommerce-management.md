## eCommerce Management Services that drive measurable growth

Running an online store is an operation, not a website. Products have to be listed accurately, stock has to be right, orders have to ship on time, returns have to be handled, and the whole thing has to keep selling while you do it. Our eCommerce management services run that operation for you: the catalog, the inventory, the orders, the store itself — so you can focus on the products and the customers while the store keeps working.

Dazzlit manages eCommerce stores on Shopify, WooCommerce and BigCommerce, and across marketplaces such as Amazon. We take over the day-to-day work that eats a store owner's time: adding and updating products, keeping stock accurate, processing orders, handling returns, and keeping the site fast and up to date. We also do the work that grows the store: merchandising, pricing, site search, checkout improvements and the analytics that show where sales are won and lost.

A well-managed store sells more for simple reasons. Products are easy to find and easy to understand. Stock is accurate, so customers are not disappointed. Checkout is fast and obvious, so fewer carts are abandoned. And the store is measured — revenue, conversion rate, average order value — so improvements go where they will make the most money. Our guide to [what is eCommerce management](/resources/guides/what-is-ecommerce-management/) explains the full scope, from catalog to checkout.

Site search and merchandising are a large part of that. Most stores earn a big share of revenue from visitors who search rather than browse, so search has to return the right products, handle misspellings and synonyms, and show useful filters. Merchandising decides what those visitors see first: bestsellers, new arrivals, bundles and offers, arranged around seasons, campaigns and stock levels. Pricing is managed too — promotions, discounts and multi-channel price consistency — so the store never undercuts itself on one channel while overcharging on another.

Whether you sell a dozen products or thousands, on one channel or five, the approach is the same: audit the store, fix what is losing sales, and run the operation to a monthly plan with clear numbers.

## What's included in our eCommerce management services: eCommerce Catalog Management; Inventory Management; Amazon Account Management; eCommerce Agency

eCommerce management covers the whole operation. Here is what is included.

### eCommerce catalog management

[eCommerce catalog management](/services/ecommerce-management/ecommerce-catalog-management/) keeps your product data accurate and complete: titles, descriptions, images, variants, prices, specifications and categories. Good product data is what makes products findable — in your own site search, in Google, and on marketplaces — and it is what convinces a buyer who cannot touch the product. We clean up existing catalogs, add new products to a consistent standard, and keep everything updated when prices, stock or suppliers change. For larger catalogs we manage product data through a PIM (product information management) system, so one source of truth feeds the website, the marketplaces and the sales team. Order management is part of the same picture: every order is tracked from checkout to delivery, so nothing is missed and nothing ships twice.

### Inventory management

[Inventory management](/services/ecommerce-management/inventory-management/) keeps stock accurate across every channel you sell on. We track levels, set reorder points, handle supplier lead times, and make sure the website never sells what the warehouse does not have. Accurate inventory stops overselling, stops cancelled orders, and stops the bad reviews that follow them. For stores that use a third-party logistics provider (3PL), we manage the relationship and the data flow between your store and the warehouse.

### Amazon account management

[Amazon account management](/services/ecommerce-management/amazon-account-management/) runs your Amazon presence as a channel, not an afterthought: listings optimised for Amazon search, pricing and promotions, advertising, reviews, and account health. Amazon has its own rules, its own search and its own customers, and managing it well means treating it as a separate store with its own plan — while keeping prices and stock consistent with your own website. Our [Amazon marketing agency](/services/marketplace-marketing/) team handles the growth side of the channel alongside the day-to-day management.

### eCommerce agency

As a full [eCommerce agency](/services/ecommerce-management/ecommerce-agency/), we cover the store end to end: the platform, the design, the marketing and the operation, in one team. That means the people who manage your catalog sit next to the people who design your product pages, run your ads and write your emails — so the store, the marketing and the fulfilment all pull in the same direction. If you only need one part of the operation managed, we scope that; if you need the whole store run, we do that too.

## Our eCommerce management process (audit → strategy → execution → reporting)

Managing a store well is a process. Ours has four stages.

### 1. Audit

We start by auditing your store: the catalog, the inventory accuracy, the order and fulfilment flow, the checkout, the site speed and the analytics. We look at where sales are lost — search that returns nothing, product pages that do not answer the buyer's questions, a checkout that leaks customers — and we measure the size of each problem. The audit sets the baseline everything else is measured against.

### 2. Strategy

From the audit we build a strategy: which parts of the operation to take over, in what order; what to fix first; and what the monthly plan covers. We agree the scope — catalog, inventory, orders, store improvements, marketing support — and the numbers we will be measured on: revenue, conversion rate, average order value, stock accuracy and fulfilment times. The strategy also covers the platform itself: whether your current setup is right, or whether a move — to Shopify, for example — would pay for itself.

### 3. Execution

Then we run the store. That means the daily work — products, stock, orders, returns, site updates — and the improvement work: fixing the product pages that underperform, improving site search and merchandising, testing the checkout, and keeping the site fast. We also work with your suppliers and your 3PL so the operation behind the website keeps pace with the storefront. Platform updates, security patches and app maintenance are handled as part of the plan, so the store stays secure and up to date without you having to track release notes or chase developers. If you need design or development work, our [web design company](/services/web-design-development/) team is in the same office.

### 4. Reporting

Every month we report on the numbers that matter: revenue, orders, conversion rate, average order value, stock accuracy, fulfilment times and return rate. We review what changed, what worked, and what to do next — which products to promote, which pages to fix, and where the store is losing sales. The report is the basis for the next month's plan, so the store keeps improving instead of standing still.

## eCommerce Management for your industry

Different stores need different management, because their products and buyers behave differently.

Fashion and apparel stores need strong merchandising, size guides and returns handling. Consumer electronics stores need detailed specifications, comparison tools and fast support. Home and garden stores need seasonal planning and large-item fulfilment. Health and beauty stores need clear ingredients, usage guidance and subscription options. Food and drink stores need expiry management, subscriptions and careful shipping. B2B stores need trade pricing, bulk ordering and account management.

Whatever you sell, the operation is built around your products and your buyers. And because our team also runs eCommerce marketing, the store and the marketing that sends it traffic are planned together — the ads, the emails and the site all point at the same products, the same offers and the same numbers.

B2B stores need their own approach. Trade customers expect bulk pricing, tax-exempt checkout, order histories and account-level catalogues, and they often buy on terms rather than by card. We set up stores that handle those requirements without complicating the experience for retail customers, and we manage the pricing and product visibility rules that keep trade and retail separate. Subscriptions and repeat ordering are handled the same way: the store is configured for the buying pattern your customers actually follow, not for a generic checkout.

## eCommerce Management pricing & packages

eCommerce management cost depends on the size of your catalog, the number of orders, and how much of the operation you hand over. Managing a small Shopify store with a few dozen products costs less than running a large multi-channel operation with Amazon, a 3PL and thousands of SKUs. That is why we quote every plan after an audit of your store rather than selling a fixed menu.

Our plans are monthly retainers for an agreed scope — catalog, inventory, orders, store improvements — with the numbers we will be measured on named in advance. You can start with one area, such as catalog or inventory, and add more as the store grows. If you want a sense of what drives the price, our article on [eCommerce management pricing](/blog/ecommerce-management-pricing/) explains the factors.

What affects the price most is the size of the operation: the number of products, the number of orders, the channels you sell on, and how much of the work you hand over. A store with a small catalog and one channel needs less management than a store with thousands of SKUs across a website, Amazon and a wholesale channel. The engagement model follows the same logic — a focused scope for one part of the operation, or a full-store retainer where we run the whole thing.
