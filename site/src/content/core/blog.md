## Blog – the short answer

The Dazzlit blog covers digital marketing as it changes: search and AI search, paid media, social, content, email and strategy, written for marketers and founders who need to know what changed and what to do about it. Each post explains what changed, why it matters and what to do next.

## Why it matters / context

Search, social and AI tools change every month, and the advice that worked a year ago can be wrong today. AI answers now sit above organic results, paid auctions shift with every platform update, and the channels buyers trust keep moving. A blog that follows those changes is worth reading; one that repeats last year's advice is not.

The blog exists to close that gap. It explains what changed, why it matters and what a business should do next — in plain language, with the measurement to back it up. The focus is on the numbers that decide budgets: leads, sales, revenue and cost per acquisition, not traffic for its own sake.

## Key factors & examples

The blog covers the full marketing funnel, from the first time a buyer hears of you to the repeat purchase. Early-funnel posts cover positioning, buyer personas and the customer journey. Mid-funnel posts cover the channels — search, paid media, social, content and email — and how the marketing mix should change as a business grows. Late-funnel posts cover measurement: KPIs, attribution, GA4, ROI, ROAS, customer acquisition cost and lifetime value, and how to report them honestly.

Two kinds of post do most of the work. The first explains a change: what Google, ChatGPT, Meta or TikTok did, who it affects and what to do about it. The second explains a decision: how to choose an agency, how to set a budget, how to judge whether a channel is working, or what to do when results stall. Both are written to be acted on, and both use first-party data and real measurement rather than opinion. Read them when a decision is coming, not as entertainment: pick the post that matches the question in front of you, and use it.

## Mistakes to avoid

The first mistake is reading about marketing instead of doing it. A post that explains a tactic is only useful if the tactic gets tested on your own traffic, with your own budget, and judged on your own numbers.

The second is taking every trend at face value. Not every platform change matters to every business, and not every new channel deserves budget. Ask whether the change affects your buyers, your funnel or your costs before you act on it.

The third is confusing a ranking with a result. A post that promises traffic rarely mentions that traffic without a working funnel is just a cost. Judge every idea by the same measures — leads, sales, revenue — and let the omnichannel picture, not a single channel, decide where the budget goes.
