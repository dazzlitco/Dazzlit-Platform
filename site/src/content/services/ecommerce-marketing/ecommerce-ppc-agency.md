## eCommerce PPC Agency: what you get

Ecommerce PPC is paid advertising with a till at the end of it. Every click costs money, every sale has a margin, and the only question that matters is whether the return beats the cost. Our job as your ecommerce PPC agency is to run your paid campaigns so that they do — profitably, and in a way you can see clearly in the numbers.

Here is what the service covers.

- **Google Shopping and Performance Max.** We build and manage the product feeds that power Shopping ads and Performance Max campaigns, so your products appear when shoppers search for them. Feed quality — titles, images, prices, availability — is treated as campaign work, not an afterthought, because a bad feed wastes spend on every click.
- **Search campaigns.** We run search ads on the terms that buy: product names, categories and the high-intent queries around them. Brand and non-brand are kept separate, so you can see what new-customer acquisition actually costs.
- **Paid social for ecommerce.** We run Meta and TikTok campaigns that find new buyers and bring back the ones who browsed but did not buy, with creative built around the product rather than the brand.
- **Retargeting and abandoned cart.** We bring back visitors who viewed products or left a cart, with ads that show the exact product they looked at and a reason to come back.
- **Tracking and measurement.** We set up conversion tracking that connects the click to the order, the revenue and the margin, so cost per acquisition (CAC) and return on ad spend are reported against real sales — not clicks.

PPC does not live on its own. A click that lands on a slow page, a confusing product page or a checkout that fails is money spent for nothing. We work with the rest of your [ecommerce marketing agency](/services/ecommerce-marketing/) team, so the ads, the store and the follow-up all pull in the same direction.

## Our eCommerce PPC Agency process

Every engagement starts with an audit: the account as it is, the feed, the tracking, the product range and the margins. We look for what is wasting spend — products advertised at a loss, campaigns with no conversion data, feeds with errors — and what is worth keeping, then agree the targets with you: the return on ad spend and the cost per acquisition the account has to hit.

From there, the work follows a fixed rhythm.

**1. Feed and tracking first.** We fix the product feed and the conversion tracking before spending more. Titles are rewritten so shoppers and the platform understand what the product is, availability is kept accurate, and every sale is tracked back to the campaign that drove it.

**2. Structure.** Campaigns are built around how your customers buy: by product category, by margin, by new versus returning customer. High-margin lines get their own budget; low-margin lines are capped. Brand and non-brand search stay separate, so acquisition cost is honest.

**3. Launch and learn.** The first weeks are for testing: products against each other, audiences against each other, creative angles against each other. We keep tests small enough to read clearly and big enough to learn from, and we do not judge a test on a handful of clicks.

**4. Optimise for profit, not clicks.** We move budget to the products and campaigns that return the most per pound spent, and we watch the numbers that decide whether a sale was worth it: conversion rate, average order value (AOV), CAC and lifetime value (LTV). A campaign can have a low cost per click and still lose money; we optimise for the sale, not the click.

**5. Scale and retain.** When a campaign beats its target consistently, we scale it — more budget, new audiences, new products — and we connect the paid work to retention: email and SMS flows for new buyers, abandoned cart recovery, and loyalty programs that turn a first order into a second. If you want the detail on the follow-up side, our guide to [SMS marketing](/resources/guides/sms-marketing/) covers how text fits into the post-purchase flow.

## Deliverables & reporting

You always know what you are paying for. Each month you receive a written report and a call to walk through it, covering spend, revenue, return on ad spend, CAC, conversion rate and AOV, broken down by campaign, product group and channel. The report also records what we tested that month, what we learned and what we are changing next.

Deliverables in a typical month include feed fixes and improvements, new or refreshed ad creative, new audience and product tests, landing page recommendations where the page is holding the campaign back, and the monthly report itself. Where reviews and user-generated content (UGC) are available, we test them in the creative — real customers talking about a product often outperform brand copy — and we report on which versions worked.

For a direct-to-consumer (DTC) brand, the report also tracks the balance between new and returning customers. Acquisition is expensive; retention is where the margin is made. We report both, so you can see whether the account is building a customer base or just buying the same sale twice.

## Results / case study

Results depend on the products, the prices, the margins and the budget, so we do not promise a fixed number. What we can show is what paid advertising has done for a real client: for a home services company, Dazzlit's paid advertising generated 450+ qualified leads and cut cost per lead by 38%. You can read the full story on our [case studies](/case-studies/) page.

The pattern behind that result is the same one we apply to every ecommerce account: clean tracking, a feed that describes the products properly, steady testing, and budget moved toward what sells profitably. The first month is usually the most expensive per sale, because the account is still learning which products and audiences convert. As winners emerge, cost per acquisition normally falls and return on ad spend rises. That is why we judge a campaign over weeks, not days, and why we report on revenue and margin rather than clicks and impressions.

## Pricing & engagement models

Ecommerce PPC has two costs, and it helps to keep them separate. The media spend goes to Google and Meta and buys the clicks; the management fee pays for the strategy, the feed work, the creative, the build and the optimisation. Both are scoped in your proposal, so there are no surprises after launch.

What affects the price is the scope of the work: how many products and campaigns you need, how much creative has to be produced each month, how many markets you advertise in, and how much testing the account needs. A single-market store with a focused range costs less to run than a multi-market catalogue with weekly creative refreshes, and we price accordingly.

We work in two main ways. Most clients are on a monthly retainer, which covers the ongoing management, testing and reporting described above. Shorter fixed-scope projects are available for a defined job, such as an account audit, a feed rebuild or a launch. Either way, the proposal states the scope, the fee and the media budget before work starts.
