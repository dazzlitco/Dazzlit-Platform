## SEM Agency: what you get

Search engine marketing — SEM — is the work of winning customers on search engines, through both paid ads and organic search. Dazzlit is an SEM agency that runs the two together: paid search campaigns that bring in leads now, and SEO that builds the rankings that bring them in for months to come.

What you get is one team managing both sides. We plan the keyword strategy once, not twice, so paid and organic never bid against each other for the same clicks. We build and manage Google Ads and Microsoft Ads campaigns, and we run the SEO work that supports them — technical fixes, content and links — in the same plan, with one report covering both.

That matters because search is where most buying journeys start. A customer searches, compares, and then decides — sometimes in minutes, sometimes over weeks. SEM makes sure your business is visible at every step: in the ads at the top of the page while they are deciding, and in the organic results they trust when they are ready.

Whether you need leads this month or a search presence that compounds over a year, the approach is the same: understand what a customer is worth, build the search strategy around intent, track every conversion, and keep improving on the evidence.

## Search Engine Marketing Agency

A [search engine marketing](/resources/guides/search-engine-marketing/) agency plans and runs your presence on search engines — Google first, and Microsoft's Bing where it makes sense for your audience. The work covers paid search (PPC), search engine optimisation (SEO) and the strategy that joins them.

Paid search buys visibility. Your ads appear when someone searches for what you sell, and you pay when they click. It is fast, controllable and measurable: campaigns can be live within days, and every click, call and sale can be traced back to a keyword. Organic search earns visibility. Your pages rank because they are relevant, useful and trusted, and the traffic they bring in costs nothing per click. It is slower to build and harder to control, but it compounds — a page that ranks well keeps bringing in customers month after month.

An SEM agency's job is to run the two as one system. That means a shared keyword plan, shared landing pages, and shared reporting, so the budget goes where it earns the most. It also means honest trade-offs: if a keyword already ranks first organically, paying for the ad on top of it may still be worth it for the extra visibility — or it may be money better spent elsewhere. The data decides, not habit.

## Paid Search Agency

As a paid search agency, we manage Google Ads and Microsoft Ads campaigns built around intent. The structure is the same discipline on both platforms: campaigns organised by product, service and location; ad groups built around tightly related keywords; ad copy written for the person searching; and landing pages that match the ad and make the next step obvious.

Bidding strategies are set by goal. Where there is enough conversion data, automated bidding — maximising conversions, or a target cost per acquisition — usually outperforms manual bids. Where the data is thin, we start with more control and hand more to automation as the evidence builds. Either way, the bids are reviewed weekly, not set and forgotten.

Match types and negative keywords do the quiet work. Exact and phrase match keep spend on searches that show real intent. A maintained negative keyword list stops the budget leaking into searches for jobs, free advice or products you do not sell. Quality Score — Google's measure of how relevant your ad and landing page are to the search — is watched closely, because a higher score means a lower cost per click and a better position.

We also run Performance Max campaigns where they fit, particularly for eCommerce and lead generation with strong conversion tracking. They reach across Search, Display, YouTube, Gmail and Maps from one campaign, but they need good creative, good audience signals and careful guardrails — otherwise they spend broadly and report vaguely. We set those guardrails before launch.

For business-to-business clients, our [b2b paid search agency](/services/ppc-management/b2b-paid-search-agency/) work applies the same discipline to longer buying cycles, where the goal is a qualified conversation rather than an instant sale.

## SEO and PPC

SEO and PPC are often sold as alternatives. They are better as partners. PPC gives you data fast: within weeks you know which keywords bring in customers, which ad messages work, and which landing pages convert. SEO takes that evidence and builds on it — the pages that convert best in paid search are the pages worth optimising for organic rankings.

It works the other way too. SEO shows you which searches your site already ranks for, so PPC does not waste money buying clicks you would have won for free — except where owning both the ad and the top organic result is worth the extra visibility. And the content built for SEO — guides, comparisons, answers to real questions — gives paid campaigns better landing pages and better Quality Scores.

The practical result is a single search strategy. One keyword plan, one set of landing pages, one report. [PPC and SEO working together](/blog/ppc-and-seo-working-together/) is how most of our clients get the best return from search: paid search for speed and testing, SEO for lasting, low-cost traffic, and both reported side by side so the budget always follows the evidence. If you are still weighing the two, [SEO vs SEM](/blog/seo-vs-sem/) explains the difference in plain terms.

## Our SEM Agency process

The work starts with discovery. We review your existing search presence — paid accounts, organic rankings, website and how enquiries come in — and agree on what a lead or a sale is worth to you. That number shapes the whole plan.

Then we research. We build the keyword plan around intent: the searches people make when they are ready to buy, the ones they make when they are comparing, and the ones that only waste budget. We look at who ranks and who bids, what their pages and ads promise, and where there is room to win. We also audit the landing pages, because search traffic is only worth buying if the page it lands on does its job.

Then we build. Paid campaigns are structured by product, service and location, with ad copy and ad extensions (assets) written for the searcher. SEO work starts with the technical fixes that matter most — speed, structure, indexing — and the content that answers the questions your customers actually ask. Conversion tracking is set up before anything goes live, covering forms, calls and purchases.

After launch, management is weekly. We check search terms, add negative keywords, test new ad copy, adjust bids, and move budget toward what produces customers. SEO work continues in the background: new content, better pages, and the links that build authority. You hear from us every month with one report covering both sides of search.

## Deliverables & reporting

A typical engagement includes the keyword plan, the paid account build or rebuild, the SEO audit and fixes, ad copy, landing page recommendations, conversion tracking setup and weekly management. [PPC in SEO](/resources/guides/ppc-in-seo/) is not an afterthought in our process — the paid data feeds the SEO plan every month.

Reporting is monthly and covers search as a whole: leads, sales, cost per lead and cost per acquisition from paid search, and traffic, rankings and conversions from organic search, side by side. We also report on click-through rate (CTR), cost per click (CPC), Quality Score, return on ad spend (ROAS) where it applies, and the keywords driving both channels, so you can see exactly what search is producing and what it costs.

You own the accounts and the data. Campaigns are built in your ad accounts, the SEO work is done on your site, and the monthly report explains what changed, what it achieved and what we plan next.

## Pricing & engagement models

There is no single price for search engine marketing, because the work depends on the size of your search presence. What affects the price is straightforward: how many campaigns are needed, how many locations and services you cover, how much SEO work the site needs, and how competitive your keywords are. A single-location business with one service costs less than a national business with dozens of services and a site that needs substantial SEO work.

Most clients work with us on a monthly retainer, which covers the weekly management of paid search, the ongoing SEO work, and the monthly reporting. A project-based setup is available when you need an account built or an SEO audit delivered and plan to manage the rest in-house. The media budget is always separate from the management fee, and it is spent in your own ad accounts — you see exactly where it goes.

Your proposal sets out the media budget, the fee and what is included, so you can judge the cost against the leads and sales search should bring in.
