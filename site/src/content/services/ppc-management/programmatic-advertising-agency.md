## Programmatic Advertising Agency: what you get

Programmatic advertising is automated media buying. Instead of negotiating with each website, TV network or radio station, software buys individual ad placements in real time, one impression at a time, based on who is seeing them. Dazzlit is a programmatic advertising agency that plans, buys and manages those campaigns for you — across display, video, connected TV, streaming audio, native and digital out-of-home.

What you get is the whole service, not just access to a buying platform. We plan the audience strategy, choose the channels and the demand-side platforms (DSPs) to buy through, build the campaigns, manage the creative, and keep optimising on performance. That includes the parts of programmatic that are easy to get wrong: brand safety, placement quality, frequency control and measurement.

Programmatic is powerful when someone is steering it and wasteful when nobody is. An unmanaged campaign will spend money on low-quality sites, show the same ad to the same person far too often, and report success in clicks that never became customers. Our job is to keep the buying tight: the right audiences, quality placements, sensible frequency, and reporting that connects spend to leads and sales.

It sits alongside paid search rather than replacing it. Google Ads and Microsoft Ads capture people who are searching right now; programmatic reaches people before they search — while they watch TV, listen to audio, read the news or browse — and brings them back when they are ready. Used together, the two cover the full journey from first seeing your brand to becoming a customer.

## Our Programmatic Advertising Agency process

The work starts with discovery. We look at what you are trying to achieve — awareness, leads, sales, store visits — and who your customers are, where they spend time, and what a customer is worth to you. That shapes everything: the channels, the audiences and the budget.

Then comes audience strategy. Programmatic targeting is built on data, and the best data is usually yours: your customer lists, your website visitors, your past buyers. We build audiences from first-party data, add lookalikes of your best customers where they make sense, and layer in contextual and demographic targeting so the ads reach people who are likely to care. Third-party data can help, but it is used carefully and only where it adds something your own data cannot.

Next we plan the channels and the buying. Display and native work well for reach and remarketing. Connected TV puts your message on streaming apps and smart TVs, targeted by household and location instead of by broadcast market — so a local business can run TV ads without paying for a whole region. Streaming audio reaches people listening to music and podcasts. Digital out-of-home covers screens in transit, gyms and retail. The mix depends on your audience and your budget, and we explain why each channel is in the plan.

Then we set up the campaigns. We choose the DSPs — platforms such as Google's Display &amp; Video 360 and The Trade Desk are the usual choices — and configure the buying: bid strategy, budgets, frequency caps, geo-targeting and brand safety settings. Creative is trafficked and checked: the right sizes, the right formats, and dynamic creative where the message should change by audience or product.

Creative deserves as much attention as targeting. The same audience will respond very differently to a clear, direct message than to a clever one, and programmatic lets us test that properly: different headlines, images and calls to action, each shown to a large enough audience to judge fairly. Where the message should change by audience or product — a different offer for past buyers than for new prospects, for example — dynamic creative builds the right version automatically, so the ad always matches the person seeing it.

Conversion tracking is set up before launch, so we can see which audiences, placements and creative produce results. After launch, management is weekly: reviewing placements and blocking poor ones, adjusting bids, refreshing creative, and shifting budget toward what works. Remarketing — or [retargeting ads](/resources/guides/retargeting-ads/) — is built in from the start, so people who visited your site see a relevant follow-up.

## Deliverables & reporting

A typical engagement includes the audience and channel plan, campaign setup across the DSPs, creative trafficking, brand safety and placement controls, and weekly management. We also handle the ongoing placement reviews: checking where ads actually appeared, blocking low-quality sites and apps, and keeping your brand off placements you would not want to be seen on.

Reporting is monthly and built around outcomes, not just impressions. You see leads, sales and cost per acquisition, broken down by channel and audience, alongside the delivery numbers that explain them: reach, frequency, click-through rate (CTR), viewability and cost per completed view where it applies. If a channel is producing impressions but no customers, the report says so, and the plan says what changes.

You also get transparency on where your ads ran. Programmatic has a reputation problem when buyers cannot see their placements; we work the other way. The placement data is yours, the accounts are yours, and the monthly report explains what we did, what it achieved and what we plan next.

For a fuller explanation of how the buying works, see our guide to [programmatic advertising](/resources/guides/programmatic-advertising/).

## Pricing & engagement models

There is no single price for programmatic advertising, because the cost depends on the channels, the audiences and the scale of the buy. What affects the price is straightforward: how many channels are in the plan, how large the audience is, whether creative needs to be produced or adapted, and how much ongoing management the campaigns need. A display and remarketing plan for a local business costs less to run than a multi-channel plan covering connected TV, audio and out-of-home across several markets.

Most clients work with us on a monthly retainer, which covers the weekly management, placement reviews, creative refreshes and reporting. A project-based setup is available when you need campaigns built for a launch or a season and plan to manage them in-house afterwards. The media budget is always separate from the management fee, and it is spent in your own accounts — you see exactly where it goes.

Your proposal sets out the media budget, the fee and what is included, so you can judge the cost against the leads and sales it should bring in.
