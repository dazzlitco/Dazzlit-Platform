## SEO Content Writing Services: what you get

SEO content writing is the writing that brings buyers to your site through search — and then turns them into enquiries. It covers the articles, guides, and landing pages that answer the questions your buyers are already typing into Google, written in a way that search engines can rank and readers can trust.

Our [SEO content writing](/resources/guides/seo-content-writing/) service includes blog writing, long-form guides, service and product pages, and the supporting pages that tie them together. Every piece starts from a content brief that sets out the reader, the question being answered, the search intent behind it, and the action it should lead to. Nothing is written to fill a quota; each piece has a job, and the job is decided before the first word is drafted.

The work is built on a content strategy, not a list of keywords. We look at what your buyers search for at each stage of the buyer journey, what your competitors already cover, and where the gaps are. Then we plan the content that fills those gaps in the order that matters most — the pages closest to a sale first, then the pages that bring people in.

## Website Content Writing Services

Your website is the centre of your content programme, and website content is what most of it is written for. That means the pages people land on: your home page, your service pages, your guides, and your blog.

Good website content does three things. It answers the question the visitor arrived with, in full and in the order they need it. It shows that you know what you are talking about, with specific detail instead of general claims. And it makes the next step obvious — whether that is contacting you, reading the next page, or downloading a guide.

We also write SEO-friendly content that respects how people actually read on a screen: clear headings, short paragraphs, and the answer near the top. Search engines reward pages that satisfy the reader, so writing for the reader and writing to rank are the same job. A content audit at the start tells us which of your existing pages are worth keeping, which need rewriting, and which subjects you have never covered at all.

Conversion content — the pages that turn readers into enquiries — gets the most attention. A guide that ranks well but never asks the reader to act is a missed opportunity, so every piece ends with a clear next step. Our guide to [SEO content marketing strategy](/blog/seo-content-marketing-strategy/) sets out how the pieces fit together across search, social, and email.

## Our SEO Content Writing Services process

Every engagement follows the same five steps.

First, discovery. We learn how your business works, who your buyers are, and what they search for. We review your analytics and your existing content, and we look at what your competitors are ranking for that you are not.

Second, strategy. We agree the topics, the formats, and the order. The plan is built around pillar pages — the main guides on your core subjects — supported by topic clusters of shorter, related articles that link back to them. That structure helps search engines understand what your site covers, and it gives readers a clear path through the subject.

Third, writing. Each piece is written from a brief, then edited for accuracy, clarity, and voice. We check facts, we cut jargon, and we make sure the piece answers the question it set out to answer. If you want to see what goes into a brief, our [content brief template](/resources/templates/content-brief-template/) shows the structure we work from.

Fourth, publishing and distribution. The piece goes live with the headings, meta description, and internal links it needs, and then it is shared through the channels where your buyers will see it — search, social, and email. Content distribution is part of the job, not an afterthought: a good article that nobody sees helps nobody.

Fifth, measurement and repurposing. We track how each piece performs and use that to decide what to write next. Strong pieces get repurposed — a guide becomes a series of social posts, a checklist, or a short video — so one piece of research works several times over.

## Deliverables & reporting

What you receive each month depends on the scope we agree, but it always includes the finished articles, ready to publish, and a report that shows what they are doing.

Each piece arrives with the headings, meta description, and internal links it needs, so it can go live without extra work from your team. Longer guides come with a summary of the sources used, so your team can check anything that needs checking. You also get the editorial calendar for the month ahead, so you always know what is being written and why. Older articles are updated when the facts change, so the library stays accurate instead of going stale.

The report covers the numbers that matter: traffic to the pages we wrote, the enquiries and sales they produced, and the cost per acquisition. We also track content ROI — what the programme costs against the revenue it brings in. Rankings, impressions, and time on page are reported too, as supporting detail, but the headline is always leads and revenue. If a piece underperforms, we say so in the report and explain what we will change — a new angle, a stronger opening, or a different format — rather than leaving it to sit.

## Pricing & engagement models

The cost of SEO content writing depends on what is involved. The main factors are how many pieces you publish each month, how long they are, and how much research each one needs. A programme of short blog posts costs less than one built around long-form guides and original research, and a single-channel programme costs less than one that feeds search, social, and email at the same time.

We work in two ways. A monthly retainer suits businesses that publish regularly and want the library to keep growing — strategy, an editorial calendar, and a steady flow of new and updated content. A project engagement suits a defined job with a clear end, such as a set of guides for a launch or a rewrite of your core service pages. Either way, your proposal sets out the scope, the deliverables, and the fee before any work starts, so there are no surprises.
