## B2B Content Marketing Services: what you get

B2B content marketing is the practice of winning business buyers with useful content instead of sales pitches. A buyer researching a supplier reads guides, compares options, and checks credentials long before they speak to anyone. The content they find during that research decides which suppliers make the shortlist — and which ones never get a call.

That is why [B2B content marketing](/resources/guides/what-is-b2b-content-marketing/) matters, and why it works differently from consumer marketing. B2B buyers are specialists. They buy in groups, not alone. Their decisions take months, and the claims in your content have to stand up to people who know the subject. Generic content does not survive that scrutiny. Specific, honest content does — and it is what builds the trust that turns a reader into a prospect.

Our B2B content marketing service covers the full programme: strategy, writing, design, distribution, and measurement. That means long-form guides and pillar pages, whitepapers, case studies, comparison pages, and the articles that bring buyers in through search. Each piece is written for a specific person at a specific stage of the buyer journey — the researcher, the evaluator, the decision maker — so the content answers the question they are asking now, and points them to the next one.

We start with a content audit of what you already have. In most B2B companies, the expertise is there but the content is not: the knowledge sits in the heads of the sales team and the specialists, and the website says very little. The audit finds the gaps, and the plan fills them in the order that matters most — the pages closest to a sale first, then the pages that bring people in.

Thought leadership is a large part of B2B content. Buyers choose suppliers they believe know more than the rest, and the way to show that is to publish thinking they cannot get anywhere else: a clear point of view, backed by experience and evidence. That is also what E-E-A-T asks for — content that shows real experience and expertise, from authors a reader can trust. Generic content fails that test. Specific content, written by people who know the subject, passes it.

## Our B2B Content Marketing process

Every engagement follows the same five steps.

First, discovery. We learn how your buyers decide, who is involved in the decision, what each person worries about, and what your competitors are saying. We talk to your sales team, because they hear the real objections every day, and we look at your analytics to see which pages already work and which ones lose people.

Second, strategy. We agree the messages that matter, the topics to cover, and the order to cover them in. This is where the content strategy takes shape: the pillar pages that anchor your core subjects, the topic clusters that support them, and the formats for each audience — guides for researchers, whitepapers for evaluators, and case studies for decision makers. Our [B2B content marketing strategy](/blog/b2b-content-marketing-strategy/) guide sets out how that planning works in practice.

Third, writing and design. Each piece is written from a brief that sets out the reader, the search intent, the key points, and the action it should lead to. Drafts go through an edit for accuracy, clarity, and voice, and your team reviews anything that touches on technical claims. Thought leadership pieces get extra care: they carry your name, so they have to be right.

Fourth, publishing and distribution. The piece goes live with the headings, meta description, and internal links it needs, and then it is shared through the channels where your buyers will see it — search, LinkedIn, and email. Content distribution is part of the job, not an afterthought. A good whitepaper that nobody sees helps nobody.

Fifth, measurement and repurposing. We track how each piece performs — who reads it, what they do next, and whether it produces pipeline — and use that to decide what to write next. Strong pieces get repurposed: a whitepaper becomes a series of articles, a checklist, and a set of social posts, so one piece of research works several times over.

## Deliverables & reporting

What you receive each month depends on the scope we agree, but it always includes the finished content, ready to publish, and a report that shows what it is doing.

The content itself arrives with the headings, meta descriptions, and internal links it needs, so it can go live without extra work from your team. Longer pieces come with a summary of the sources and claims used, so your specialists can check anything that needs checking. You also get the editorial calendar for the month ahead, so you always know what is being written and why.

The report covers the numbers that matter: traffic to the pages we wrote, the enquiries they produced, the qualified opportunities in the pipeline, and the cost per acquisition. We also track content ROI — what the programme costs against the revenue it brings in — because B2B content has to pay for itself like any other channel. Rankings and engagement are reported too, as supporting detail, but the headline is always pipeline and revenue.

## Pricing & engagement models

The cost of B2B content marketing depends on what is involved. The main factors are how much you publish each month, how much research each piece requires, and how many channels the content has to serve. B2B content usually needs more research than consumer content, because the buyers are specialists and the claims have to stand up. A programme of articles costs less than one built around whitepapers, original research, and long-form guides.

We work in two ways. A monthly retainer suits businesses that publish regularly and want the library to keep growing — strategy, an editorial calendar, and a steady flow of new and updated content. A project engagement suits a defined job with a clear end, such as a whitepaper, a set of guides for a launch, or a rewrite of your core service pages. Either way, your proposal sets out the scope, the deliverables, and the fee before any work starts, so there are no surprises.
