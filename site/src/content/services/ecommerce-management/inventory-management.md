## Inventory Management: what you get

Inventory management is the part of ecommerce nobody sees and everybody feels. When stock levels are wrong, customers order products you do not have, bestsellers sell out without warning, and money sits in slow-moving lines while the lines people want are empty. When it is right, the store just works: orders go out on time, the website shows what is actually available, and you can plan buying with confidence.

Our inventory management service runs that part of your store for you. We keep the product catalog clean, stock levels accurate across every channel, and orders moving from the shelf to the customer without manual firefighting. The work covers the full ecommerce operation, not just the counting.

- **Product catalog and product data.** We manage your product information — titles, descriptions, images, variants, prices and attributes — in one place, so the website, the marketplaces and the warehouse all work from the same truth. Clean product data, often held in a PIM (product information management) system, is what makes everything downstream reliable.
- **Stock control.** We track inventory across your store, marketplaces and warehouses, set reorder points, and flag slow movers before they tie up cash. The aim is simple: never oversell, rarely stock out, and know what is on the shelf at any moment.
- **Order management.** We run the ecommerce order flow end to end — order capture, picking, packing, shipment and tracking — and we handle the exceptions, such as split shipments and address problems, before they become complaints.
- **Fulfillment and 3PL.** Whether you fulfill in-house or through a third-party logistics (3PL) partner, we coordinate the handover, monitor dispatch times and keep the tracking information flowing back to the customer. If you are weighing the switch, our guide to [3PL](/resources/guides/3pl/) explains how third-party fulfillment works and when it makes sense.
- **Returns.** We run a returns process that protects the customer relationship and the margin: clear rules, fast inspection, quick restocking or refund, and reporting on why products come back.

Good inventory management shows up in the numbers your customers care about: accurate stock on the product page, honest delivery promises, and orders that arrive when you said they would.

## eCommerce Order Management

An ecommerce order is a promise, and the order management system is what keeps it. From the moment a customer checks out, the system has to confirm the order, reserve the stock, pick and pack it, book the shipment, send the tracking, and handle anything that goes wrong along the way. When any of those steps is manual or disconnected, orders slip — and the customer finds out before you do.

We set up and run the order management system for your store, whether it is the order system built into your platform — Shopify, WooCommerce or BigCommerce — or a dedicated order management system for ecommerce that sits across several channels. The system connects the store, the warehouse and the carriers, so every ecommerce order is tracked from checkout to delivery in one place.

The practical work includes setting the rules that decide how orders are fulfilled: which warehouse ships what, when an order splits, what happens when stock is short, and how backorders are handled. We also manage the order and shipment paperwork — packing slips, labels, customs forms where needed — and the customer messages that go with each step. A customer who knows where their order is rarely needs to ask.

For the detail on how orders move through a warehouse, our guide to [order fulfillment](/resources/guides/order-fulfillment/) walks through the process step by step.

## eCommerce Inventory Management

Ecommerce inventory management is the discipline of keeping the right products, in the right quantities, in the right places. It sounds simple; in practice it means reconciling what the website says, what the warehouse holds, what the suppliers can deliver and what customers actually buy — across every channel you sell on.

We run the cycle for you. That starts with counting and reconciling: regular stock checks that catch the drift between the system and the shelf, and corrections when the two disagree. It continues with forecasting: using sales history, seasonality and supplier lead times to set reorder points, so you buy before you run out and not after. And it ends with reporting: stock accuracy, stockouts, overstocks, sell-through and the cash tied up in inventory, reviewed with you every month.

Software matters, but it is not the whole answer. The right ecommerce inventory management software gives you one view of stock across channels and automates the routine work — low-stock alerts, purchase orders, channel sync. We help you choose and configure it, and our article on [ecommerce inventory management software](/blog/ecommerce-inventory-management-software/) explains what to look for. The rest is process: someone has to act on the alerts, chase the supplier and fix the listing when the data is wrong. That someone is us.

Inventory also connects directly to merchandising and pricing. Slow-moving lines get flagged for promotion or clearance; fast movers get protected stock and better placement. Site search and checkout depend on it too: a search result that leads to an out-of-stock product is a sale lost, and a checkout that accepts an order for stock you do not have is a refund waiting to happen.

## Our Inventory Management process

Every engagement starts with an audit of the operation as it is: the catalog, the stock accuracy, the order flow, the fulfillment setup and the tools. We find where orders break, where stock data goes stale and where time is being spent on work a system should do.

From there, the work follows a fixed rhythm.

**1. Clean the data.** We fix the product catalog first — duplicates, missing variants, wrong prices, images that do not match. Clean product data is the foundation; nothing downstream works without it.

**2. Connect the systems.** We link the store, the inventory system, the warehouse or 3PL, and the carriers, so stock and orders flow between them without retyping. Where a platform cannot do something, we say so and work around it rather than pretending it will.

**3. Set the rules.** Reorder points, safety stock, fulfillment rules, returns rules and the exceptions process are written down and agreed with you. Rules that live in one person's head do not survive a holiday.

**4. Run the operation.** We manage the daily flow — orders, stock movements, supplier orders, returns — and report weekly on what moved, what is short and what needs a decision.

**5. Improve monthly.** Each month we review the numbers: stock accuracy, dispatch times, returns and the cash in inventory. We fix the biggest problem first, then the next. Small, steady improvements beat a one-off overhaul that nobody maintains.

To keep the routine work visible, our [inventory management template](/resources/templates/inventory-management-template/) gives you the structure we use for stock checks and reorder planning.

## Deliverables & reporting

Each month you receive a written report covering the numbers that run the store: stock accuracy, stockouts and overstocks, order accuracy, dispatch times, returns, conversion rate and revenue. The report also records what was fixed, what is still a problem and what changes next — so you can see the operation improving, not just the sales line.

Deliverables in a typical month include the reconciled stock position, the reorder plan, the supplier orders placed, the returns summary and the monthly report itself. You also get a named contact who knows your catalog and your suppliers, and a weekly check-in while the operation is being stabilised. Analytics are part of the package: we track which products sell, which sit, and which channels earn their shelf space, so buying decisions are based on evidence rather than habit.

## Pricing & engagement models

Ecommerce management is priced by the size and complexity of the operation: the number of products, the number of sales channels, the order volume, and how much of the operation you hand over. A small single-channel store costs less to manage than a multi-channel business with thousands of products and several warehouses.

We work in two main ways. Most clients are on a monthly retainer, which covers the ongoing management of the catalog, inventory, orders and returns, plus the monthly reporting. Shorter fixed-scope projects are available for a defined job, such as a catalog clean-up, a platform migration or setting up the order management system. Either way, the proposal states the scope and the fee before work starts, and the fee reflects your actual operation rather than a flat rate.
