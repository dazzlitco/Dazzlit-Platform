## Shopify Development Services: what you get

Shopify is a strong choice for selling online because it handles the hard parts of ecommerce — secure checkout, payments, and hosting — while leaving the design and the customer experience up to you. The difference between a Shopify store that sells and one that does not is how it is designed and built.

Our Shopify development services build custom Shopify stores that are fast, easy to manage, and designed to turn visits into orders. You get a store designed around your products and your customers, built on a custom theme, with the apps and integrations your business needs and nothing it does not. Every project includes strategy, UX and UI design, development, product setup, technical SEO, and launch. The design is responsive and mobile-first, because most shoppers arrive on a phone, and it meets accessibility (WCAG) standards so everyone can use it.

Your team gets a store they can run day to day: adding products, managing orders, and updating pages without touching code. And because the build is clean and documented, future changes are straightforward. If you are planning the store yourself first, our [how to build a shopify store](/blog/how-to-build-a-shopify-store/) article explains the steps involved, and our [shopify web design](/resources/guides/shopify-web-design/) guide covers the design decisions behind a store that sells.

## Shopify Website Development

Shopify website development covers the full build of your store, from the first design to the day it starts taking orders.

**Custom theme.** We build a custom theme, so the store looks like your brand and carries only the code it needs. A bought theme often carries features you will never use, and that extra code slows the store down. A custom theme is leaner, faster, and easier to change later.

**Products and collections.** We set up your products, variants, collections, and filters, so customers can find what they want quickly. Product pages are designed to sell: clear images, honest descriptions, reviews where you have them, and a checkout path with as little friction as possible.

**Checkout, payments, and shipping.** We configure checkout, payment providers, taxes, and shipping rules for the markets you serve, and we test the full journey from product page to order confirmation. A smooth checkout is where most lost sales are won back, so we check it on real devices and connections.

**Apps and integrations.** We choose apps carefully, preferring well-maintained ones with a clear purpose, and we connect the tools your business already uses, such as email marketing, accounting, or a CRM. Fewer, better apps means a faster store and fewer conflicts.

**Migration.** If you are moving from another platform, we move your products, customers, and order history, keep your URLs working with redirects, and check that search visibility carries over. The technical SEO foundations — clean URLs, structured data, sitemaps, and analytics — are set up before launch, and the store is tested against Core Web Vitals, the loading and stability measures Google uses.

## Bigcommerce Developer

Shopify is not the only platform for selling online, and the right choice depends on your products, your team, and how you sell. We also build stores on BigCommerce, and we help you choose between the two before any work starts.

BigCommerce suits businesses that need more built-in features without relying on apps: larger catalogues, complex product options, and business-to-business selling are areas where it is often a good fit. Shopify suits businesses that want a simpler setup, a large app ecosystem, and a checkout that customers already know. Both are hosted platforms, so security, updates, and hosting are handled for you, and both can be designed and built to the same standard.

If you already sell on BigCommerce and need a redesign, a rebuild, or a migration to Shopify, we handle that too: products and customers move across, redirects keep your search rankings intact, and the new store is tested before it goes live. The decision comes from discovery, where we look at what you sell, how you sell it, and what the store needs to do now and in a year or two, and choose the simplest platform that covers both.

## Our Shopify Development Services process

We follow a clear process, so you always know what is happening and when the store will launch.

**Discover.** We start with your products, your customers, and your goals. We audit your current store if you have one, look at competitors, and map the pages and features the new store needs. Technical requirements, such as payments, shipping, and integrations, are agreed here, along with how success will be measured.

**Design.** We plan the information architecture first, then design wireframes that show the layout of each key page. From there we build visual prototypes, so you can see the real design before anything is coded. You review and approve each stage.

**Build.** We develop the approved design into a custom Shopify theme in a staging environment you can review. Products are set up, apps and integrations are configured, and technical SEO, analytics, and accessibility are set up. We test across browsers and devices, and check site speed and Core Web Vitals.

**Launch and improve.** We run through a launch checklist covering redirects, sitemaps, and testing, then move the store live. In the first weeks we watch performance closely, fix anything that needs fixing, and start monthly reporting. After launch, we keep improving the pages and the checkout that matter most.

## Pricing & engagement models

Shopify development costs vary with scope. The main factors are the number of products, how much of the design and theme is custom, what the store connects to, and whether you need product data work or a migration from an existing store. A focused store on a custom theme costs less than a large build with custom features, complex shipping rules, and integrations.

We work in three ways. A **project** covers the design and build of the store for a fixed, agreed fee. A **retainer** covers ongoing work after launch, such as new pages, merchandising, and conversion improvements. A **phased** engagement splits a larger build into stages, so you can launch the core store first and add features over time. Your proposal sets out the scope, the fee, and the timeline in writing before work starts.
