## eCommerce Website Development: what you get

An ecommerce website has one job above all others: turn visits into orders. Everything else — the design, the product pages, the checkout, the speed — exists to make that happen. A store that looks good but loads slowly, hides its prices, or makes checkout hard will not sell, however much traffic it gets.

Our ecommerce website development service builds online stores that are fast, easy to manage, and designed to sell. You get a store designed around your products and your customers, built on the platform that fits your business, with the payment, shipping, and marketing tools you need. Every project includes strategy, UX and UI design, development, product setup, technical SEO, and launch. The design is responsive and mobile-first, because most shoppers arrive on a phone, and it meets accessibility (WCAG) standards so everyone can use it.

Your team gets a store they can run day to day: adding products, managing orders, and updating pages without touching code. And because the build is clean and documented, future changes are straightforward. Our [ecommerce web design](/resources/guides/ecommerce-web-design/) guide covers the design principles behind a store that sells, and our [website project plan template](/resources/templates/website-project-plan-template/) helps you prepare the product and content details before the build starts.

## eCommerce Web Design Company

Design decides whether a shopper stays, and development decides whether they can buy. As an ecommerce web design company, we do both in one team, so the store is designed to sell and built to work.

The design starts with how people shop. The homepage makes the range and the offer clear. Category pages help shoppers narrow down quickly, with filters that work on a phone. Product pages show clear images, honest descriptions, prices, delivery information, and reviews where you have them. The cart and checkout are kept short, with guest checkout available, because every extra step loses orders.

Trust is designed in, not added later. Secure payment marks, clear returns information, and real contact details sit where shoppers look for them. The visual style matches your brand, with consistent typography and colour, and photography that shows the products properly.

Behind the design, the build is focused on speed and reliability. Pages load quickly, the store works on every device, and the checkout is tested on real phones and connections. We test against Core Web Vitals, the loading and stability measures Google uses, because a slow store loses shoppers before they reach the product. Our [ui ux design](/resources/guides/ui-ux-design/) guide explains the design principles we follow, and the wider service sits under our [web design company](/services/web-design-development/) offering, where design, development, search, and content are planned as one strategy.

## eCommerce Web Development Services

Ecommerce web development covers the full build of your store: the platform, the theme, the products, the checkout, and the connections to the tools your business runs on.

**Platform.** We build on Shopify, WooCommerce (WordPress), BigCommerce, and Magento (Adobe Commerce). The choice depends on your catalogue size, how you sell, and what your team needs to manage. Shopify suits most stores that want a simple setup and a large app ecosystem. WooCommerce suits businesses already on WordPress that want full control. BigCommerce suits larger catalogues and business-to-business selling. Magento suits complex stores with custom requirements. We help you choose before any work starts, and we are honest when a simpler platform will do the job.

**Theme and products.** We build a custom theme, so the store looks like your brand and carries only the code it needs. Products, variants, collections, and filters are set up so customers can find what they want quickly, and product data is structured so search engines can understand it.

**Checkout, payments, and shipping.** We configure checkout, payment providers, taxes, and shipping rules for the markets you serve, and we test the full journey from product page to order confirmation. A smooth checkout is where most lost sales are won back.

**Integrations.** We connect the tools your business already uses, such as email marketing, accounting, stock management, or a CRM, choosing well-maintained apps and integrations and keeping the number small, so the store stays fast. If you are moving from another platform, we migrate products, customers, and order history, keep your URLs working with redirects, and check that search visibility carries over.

## Magento Website Development

Magento, now Adobe Commerce, is built for larger and more complex stores. It suits businesses with big catalogues, multiple stores or currencies, custom pricing, or business-to-business selling that a simpler platform cannot handle well.

We build and rebuild Magento stores with a custom theme, structured product data, and the integrations the business needs, such as stock, accounting, or a CRM. Performance is a central part of the build: caching, image optimisation, and code kept lean, so the store loads quickly even with a large catalogue. Security and updates are handled properly, with backups and a staging environment for testing changes before they go live.

Magento is not the right choice for every store. It needs more ongoing care than a hosted platform, and a smaller store will usually do better on Shopify or WooCommerce. We say so when that is the case, because the platform should fit the business, not the other way around. If you already run a Magento store that has grown slow or hard to manage, we can rebuild or clean it up, migrating your data safely and keeping your search rankings intact with proper redirects.

## Our eCommerce Website Development process

We follow a clear process, so you always know what is happening and when the store will launch.

**Discover.** We start with your products, your customers, and your goals. We audit your current store if you have one, look at competitors, and map the pages and features the new store needs. Technical requirements, such as payments, shipping, and integrations, are agreed here, along with how success will be measured.

**Design.** We plan the information architecture first, then design wireframes that show the layout of each key page. From there we build visual prototypes, so you can see the real design before anything is coded. You review and approve each stage.

**Build.** We develop the approved design into a working store in a staging environment you can review. Products are set up, apps and integrations are configured, and technical SEO, analytics, and accessibility are set up. We test across browsers and devices, and check site speed and Core Web Vitals. Our [website launch checklist](/resources/checklists/website-launch-checklist/) and [website accessibility checklist](/resources/checklists/website-accessibility-checklist/) cover what we check before the store goes live.

**Launch and improve.** We run through the launch list, move the store live, and watch performance closely in the first weeks. After launch, we report each month and keep improving the product pages, the checkout, and the collections that matter most.

The report also lists what we are improving next, so the store keeps getting better instead of standing still.

## Pricing & engagement models

Ecommerce development costs vary with scope. The main factors are the number of products, how much of the design and theme is custom, what the store connects to, and whether you need product data work or a migration from an existing store. A focused store on a standard platform costs less than a large build with custom features, complex shipping rules, and integrations.

We work in three ways. A **project** covers the design and build of the store for a fixed, agreed fee. A **retainer** covers ongoing work after launch, such as new pages, merchandising, and conversion improvements. A **phased** engagement splits a larger build into stages, so you can launch the core store first and add features over time. Your proposal sets out the scope, the fee, and the timeline in writing before work starts.
