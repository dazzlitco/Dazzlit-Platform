## Web Development: what you get

Web development is the work that turns a design into a website people can actually use: fast, secure, and easy to manage. It covers everything behind the pages you see, from the code that renders the layout to the content management system, the forms, the integrations, and the hosting.

Our web development service builds websites that are ready for real traffic and real customers. You get a site developed around your goals, on a CMS your team can update, with technical SEO, analytics, and performance built in from the start. Every project includes discovery, UX and UI design, development, content, testing, and launch. The build is responsive and mobile-first, meets accessibility (WCAG) standards, and is tested against Core Web Vitals, the loading and stability measures Google uses to judge page experience.

Whether you need a marketing site, a custom web application, or a rebuild of something that has grown messy over the years, the approach is the same: understand the job the site has to do, design for the people who will use it, and build it so it can grow. ## Website Development

Website development covers the full build of a site, from the first line of code to the day it goes live. It has three parts, and all three matter.

**Front-end development** is what visitors see and touch: the layout, the navigation, the forms, and the interactive elements. Good front-end work is fast and accessible. Pages load quickly, work on phones and desktops, and can be used with a keyboard or a screen reader. We build with clean, maintainable code, so future changes are straightforward rather than fragile.

**Back-end development** is what powers the site behind the scenes: the CMS, the database, user accounts, and the connections to other tools. This is where content is managed, forms are processed, and data flows to your CRM or email platform. A well-built back end is secure and reliable, with backups and updates handled properly.

**Content and integrations** are what make the site useful day to day. We set up the pages, the blog, and the media library, and we connect the tools your business already uses, such as booking systems, payment providers, or analytics. Hosting is configured for speed and security, with SSL and caching in place.

The result is a website your team can run without calling a developer for every change, and your customers can use without friction. For examples of what a finished build can look like, see our [web development website inspiration](/resources/examples/web-development-website-inspiration/) collection.

## Web Design and Development

Web design and web development are two halves of the same job, and they work best when they are planned together. Design decides how the site looks, feels, and guides visitors. Development decides how it works, how fast it loads, and how easy it is to manage. When the two are done by separate teams without talking to each other, you get designs that are hard to build and builds that do not match the design.

We do both in one team. The designer and the developer work from the same plan, so the design is built to be fast and accessible from the start, and the build stays faithful to the design. Wireframes and prototypes come first, so you can see and test the layout before anything is coded. Then development turns the approved design into a working site, with the CMS, forms, and integrations included.

This matters for conversion-centered design. A page that looks good but loads slowly or hides its call to action will not produce enquiries. A page that is technically perfect but confusing to read will not either. The two disciplines have to meet in the middle, and that is easiest when one team owns both. Our [web design vs web development](/blog/web-design-vs-web-development/) article explains the difference in more detail.

## Custom Web Development

Some websites need more than a standard CMS setup. Custom web development is for sites with specific requirements: a booking flow, a customer portal, a product configurator, a complex integration, or a design that no theme can deliver.

We start by mapping what the custom work has to do and what it has to connect to. Then we build it as part of the site, rather than bolting it on afterwards, so it is fast, secure, and maintainable. Custom features are documented, so your team or any future developer can understand how they work. We also plan for maintenance: code is kept up to date, dependencies are reviewed, and changes are tested in staging before they go live.

Custom development is not always the right answer. If a standard CMS and a well-chosen set of tools can do the job, we will say so, because a simpler build costs less to run and is easier to maintain. The decision comes from the discovery stage, where we look at what the site must do now and what it may need in a year or two, and choose the simplest build that covers both.

## Our Web Development process

We follow a clear process, so you always know what is happening and when the site will launch.

**Discover.** We start with your goals, your audience, and the job the site has to do. We audit your current site if you have one, look at competitors, and map the pages and features the new site needs. Technical requirements, such as integrations and hosting, are agreed here, along with how success will be measured.

**Design.** We plan the information architecture first, then design wireframes that show the layout of each key page. From there we build visual prototypes, so you can see the real design before anything is coded. You review and approve each stage.

**Build.** We develop the approved design into a working website in a staging environment you can review. Content is added, forms and integrations are connected, and technical SEO, analytics, and accessibility are set up. We test across browsers and devices, and check site speed and Core Web Vitals.

**Launch and improve.** We run through a launch checklist covering redirects, sitemaps, and testing, then move the site live. In the first weeks we watch performance closely, fix anything that needs fixing, and start monthly reporting. After launch, we keep improving the pages and features that matter most.

## Pricing & engagement models

Web development costs vary with scope. The main factors are the number of pages, how much of the design and build is custom, what the site connects to, and whether you need new copy or content. A focused marketing site costs less than a custom build with a portal, booking flow, or complex integrations.

We work in three ways. A **project** covers the design and build of the site for a fixed, agreed fee. A **retainer** covers ongoing work after launch, such as new features, content, and performance improvements. A **phased** engagement splits a larger build into stages, so you can launch the core site first and add features over time. Your proposal sets out the scope, the fee, and the timeline in writing before work starts.
