## Web Design for Law Firms: what you get

Most people choose a lawyer online before they ever make a call. They search for a practice area, open two or three firm websites, and judge each one in seconds. A law firm website has to do three jobs at once: show that the firm is credible, make it easy to find the right practice area, and turn a visit into a consultation request.

Our law firm web design service builds websites that do all three. You get a site designed around how clients actually choose a lawyer, built on a content management system your team can update, and set up so search engines can find it. Every project includes strategy, UX and UI design, copywriting support, development, technical SEO foundations, and launch. The design is responsive and mobile-first, because most legal searches start on a phone, and it meets accessibility (WCAG) standards so every visitor can use it.

The result is a website that works as hard as your intake team: clear practice-area pages, attorney profiles that build trust, visible ways to call or request a consultation, and reporting that shows how many enquiries the site produces.

## Lawyer Web Design

Lawyer web design is different from design for other businesses because the decision to hire a lawyer is personal and often urgent. A visitor may be dealing with an injury, a dispute, or a family matter. They need to feel confident in the firm quickly, and they need a clear next step.

That is why we design law firm sites around trust and clarity. The homepage states what the firm does and who it helps. Practice-area pages explain each service in plain language, with the questions clients actually ask. Attorney profiles show experience and approach, not just job titles. Reviews and case outcomes, where the firm can share them, sit close to the contact points rather than buried on a separate page.

Calls to action are direct and easy to find on every page: call the firm, request a consultation, or send a message. Contact forms are short, because long forms lose people who are ready to talk. We also plan the site's information architecture around search, so each practice area has its own page that can rank for the terms people use, such as personal injury lawyer or family law attorney in your city.

The visual style stays calm and professional. Clean layouts, readable type, and restrained colour build confidence, while strong photography of the real team and offices helps the firm feel human. For more on how the site fits into the firm's wider marketing, see our [law firm marketing](/industries/law-firms/) page.

## Law Firm Website Development

Good design only works if the build behind it is solid. Our law firm website development covers the technical side: fast, secure, accessible websites that your team can manage without calling a developer for every change.

We build on WordPress, a CMS that gives your team control over pages, blog posts, and attorney profiles. The site is responsive by default, so it works on phones, tablets, and desktops, and we test it against Core Web Vitals — the loading, interactivity, and stability measures Google uses to judge page experience. Site speed matters for both rankings and enquiries: a slow page loses visitors before they read a word.

Accessibility is built in, not added later. We follow WCAG guidance on contrast, keyboard navigation, form labels, and readable structure, so the site works for visitors using screen readers or other assistive technology. That also protects the firm, since accessibility complaints against law firm websites are a real risk.

Behind the scenes, we handle hosting setup, security hardening, and technical SEO: clean URLs, structured data for the firm and its attorneys, sitemaps, and analytics. Intake forms connect to your email or CRM, and call tracking can be added so you know which pages produce calls. The site is tested on real devices before launch, and your team gets training so they can publish updates themselves.

## Our Law Firm Web Design process

We follow a clear process, so you always know what is happening and when the site will launch.

**Discover.** We start with your goals, your practice areas, and your best clients. We audit your current site, look at what competing firms are doing, and map the pages the new site needs. This is also where we agree how success will be measured, usually consultation requests, calls, and form submissions.

**Design.** We plan the information architecture first, then design wireframes that show the layout of each key page. From there we build visual prototypes, so you can see the real design before anything is coded. You review and approve each stage, so there are no surprises at launch.

**Build.** We develop the approved design into a working website, add your content, and set up forms, analytics, and technical SEO. The site is built in a staging environment you can review, and we test it across browsers and devices.

**Launch and improve.** We check the launch list, move the site live, and monitor it in the first weeks. After launch we report on performance each month and keep improving the pages that matter most.

## Deliverables & reporting

When the project ends, you own the website, the design files, and the content. Your team gets a CMS they can use, a short training session, and written guidance on updating pages and publishing posts.

What you receive depends on scope, but a typical law firm project includes the designed and built website, practice-area and attorney pages, contact and consultation forms, analytics and search console setup, and a launch checklist that covers redirects, sitemaps, and testing.

Reporting starts at launch. Each month we send a report covering the numbers that matter to a firm: calls, form submissions, and consultation requests, plus traffic, search visibility, and which pages produce enquiries. The report also lists what we are improving next, so the site keeps getting better instead of standing still.

## Pricing & engagement models

Law firm website costs vary with scope. The main factors are the number of pages, how much of the design is custom, whether the firm needs new copy or photography, and what the site connects to, such as intake software or a CRM. A focused site for a small firm costs less than a large site with many practice areas, custom tools, and integrations.

We work in three ways. A **project** covers the design and build of the site for a fixed, agreed fee. A **retainer** covers ongoing work after launch, such as new pages, content, and conversion improvements. A **phased** engagement splits a larger build into stages, so the firm can launch the core site first and add practice areas over time. Your proposal sets out the scope, the fee, and the timeline in writing before work starts.
