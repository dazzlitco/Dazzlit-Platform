## Digital Marketing Consulting: what you get

Digital marketing consulting is an outside view of your marketing, from people who run campaigns every day. We look at your channels, your website, your data and your competitors, then tell you what is working, what is wasting money and what to do next. The result is a written plan you can act on, whether you run it in-house, hand it to us, or split the work between the two.

A consulting engagement starts with your goals, not with a channel. More leads, more sales, a launch in a new market, a website that converts better: the goal decides the marketing mix. From there we map the customer journey, from the first search to the signed contract, and check every step for gaps. That map covers the marketing funnel end to end, from awareness to repeat purchase, and shows where each channel earns its place.

The plan also covers your buyer personas, your brand positioning and the numbers the plan will be judged on. We agree KPIs for each stage of the funnel, from traffic and enquiries to cost per acquisition (CAC), return on ad spend (ROAS) and lifetime value (LTV). Where the data allows, we set up attribution in GA4, so you can see which channels bring in the customers, not just the clicks. And because first-party data matters more every year, we look at how you collect and use your own customer data, from your website and your CRM to your email list.

You get the thinking of a full [digital marketing agency](/) without committing to a full retainer first. Consulting suits businesses that want a clear direction before they spend, teams that need a second opinion on an existing plan, and companies entering a new market who need to know how that market behaves before they invest.

## Marketing Consulting Firms

There are many marketing consulting firms, and they are not all the same. Some sell a thick report and leave. Some only advise on one channel. A useful firm does three things: it understands your market, it has run the channels it recommends, and it stays accountable for the results of its advice.

When you compare firms, ask what their consultants have actually run. A plan written by someone who has managed search, paid media and content in your market is worth more than a generic framework. Ask how they measure success, and whether they will report on revenue and return on investment (ROI), not just traffic. And ask what happens after the plan is delivered: who builds it, who runs it, and who answers for it.

Dazzlit works as both consultant and agency. We advise on the strategy, and we can also run the channels in it, across search, paid media, social, content and web. That keeps the plan honest: we only recommend work we are prepared to deliver, and we report on the same numbers we planned against. It also means the plan is written for the real world, with budgets, timelines and people attached, rather than as a document that sits on a shelf.

## Our Digital Marketing Consulting process

Every engagement follows the same four steps, sized to your brief.

Discover. We audit what you have: your website, your analytics, your ad accounts, your content and your competitors. We interview your team, review your customer data and check how your brand is positioned against the alternatives your buyers consider. The audit ends with a clear picture of where you stand and where the gaps are, including the quick wins that can be fixed first.

Plan. We build the strategy: the channels, the budget split, the timeline and the targets. The plan sets out the marketing mix for each stage of the funnel, the audiences and buyer personas for each channel, and the KPIs that will tell us whether it is working. You see the reasoning behind every recommendation, including the channels we suggest you skip and why. Nothing in the plan is there by habit.

Build. We set up what the plan needs: tracking and attribution in GA4, landing pages, campaign structures, content calendars and reporting dashboards. If your team will run the plan, we hand over the setup with the documentation and training to use it, so nothing is lost when we step back.

Grow. We review performance every month against the agreed KPIs, and adjust the plan as the data comes in. Channels that beat their targets get more budget; channels that do not are fixed or paused. The plan is a living document, not a one-off report, and it changes as your market changes.

## Deliverables & reporting

You always know what you are getting. A consulting engagement delivers a written strategy document, the audit findings behind it, a channel-by-channel plan with budgets and timelines, and a reporting dashboard built around your KPIs. If the engagement covers more than one market, the plan shows what changes by market and what stays the same, so local teams work from one direction.

Reporting is monthly, in plain language. Each report shows leads, sales, revenue and cost per acquisition, with the contribution of each channel. We include what changed, what we did about it and what happens next. Your consultant walks you through the report, so the numbers are always explained by a person who knows your account, and you can ask questions as you go.

We also report on the customer journey, not just the last click. Attribution in GA4 shows how search, paid media, social and content work together, so a channel that starts the journey gets the credit it deserves. And we keep your first-party data at the centre of the reporting, so the picture improves as your data grows and you rely less on third-party tracking.

## Pricing & engagement models

The cost of consulting depends on the size of the audit, the number of channels in the plan and the markets it covers. A focused review of one channel costs less than a full omnichannel strategy for several countries. We scope every engagement to your brief before work starts, so you know the price and the deliverables in advance, and there are no surprises in the first invoice.

We work in three ways. A one-off audit and strategy gives you the plan and the setup, and your team runs it. A monthly retainer keeps the consultant on your account, reviewing performance and updating the plan as the market changes. And a project engagement covers a defined piece of work, such as a market-entry plan or a website and tracking rebuild, with a fixed scope and a fixed end date.

Most clients start with an audit and strategy, then move to a retainer once the plan is running. That keeps the cost tied to the work, and the work tied to the results. Whichever model you choose, the proposal sets out the scope, the deliverables and the fee before you commit.
