## Email Marketing Agency: what you get

Email is the channel you own. Unlike search rankings or social feeds, your list cannot be taken away by an algorithm change, and it reaches people who have already said they want to hear from you. An email marketing agency turns that list into revenue: the right message, to the right people, at the right time, measured against sales.

What you get is a full programme, not just a newsletter. We start with your list and your goals, then build the [email marketing campaigns](/resources/guides/email-marketing-campaigns/) around them: a welcome series for new subscribers, lifecycle flows for buyers and lapsed customers, promotional campaigns for launches and seasons, and a regular newsletter people actually open. Each campaign is built for a segment of your list, not blasted to everyone at once.

The work covers strategy, creative and data. Strategy sets the calendar, the segments and the goals for each campaign. Creative covers the design and the copy, written for your buyer personas and your brand positioning. Data covers the list itself: how it is collected, cleaned and segmented, and how first-party data from your website and your CRM feeds the campaigns. And behind all of it sits deliverability: the setup and list hygiene that decide whether your emails reach the inbox at all.

Email also fits into the wider marketing mix. It is the follow-up channel: it turns search traffic into subscribers, paid clicks into buyers, and one-time buyers into repeat customers. We plan it as part of an omnichannel customer journey, not as a separate job, so the [email marketing](/resources/guides/email-marketing/) programme shares its goals, its audiences and its reporting with the rest of your marketing. That also keeps your brand positioning consistent: the same message, in the same voice, wherever a customer meets you.

## Email Marketing Firm

There are many email marketing firms, and the difference between them is what they take responsibility for. Some will design a template and leave you to run it. A full-service firm takes the whole programme: strategy, automation, design, copywriting, deliverability, testing and reporting, and answers for the revenue it produces.

When you compare firms, ask what is included. Does the fee cover automation and lifecycle flows, or only one-off campaigns? Does it cover list management and deliverability, or only design? Does the firm report on revenue and return on investment (ROI), or only on opens and clicks? And ask which email platforms they work with, because the right [email marketing software](/blog/email-marketing-software/) depends on the size of your list, your store or CRM, and the automation you need.

Also ask how the firm handles your data. A good firm works only with permission-based lists: customers, subscribers and enquiries who opted in. It does not buy lists, and it does not send cold email to strangers, because that damages deliverability and your brand. Dazzlit works only with lists built from real opt-ins, and we clean and segment them properly before a single campaign goes out.

## Our Email Marketing Agency process

Every engagement follows the same four steps, sized to your brief.

Discover. We audit what you have: your list, your platform, your past campaigns, your sign-up forms and your competitors. We check deliverability, from authentication to list hygiene, and we look at how your list is segmented and what your subscribers actually do. We also review your customer data, so the programme is built on what your buyers buy and when, not on guesswork.

Plan. We build the strategy: the campaigns, the automated flows, the segments and the calendar, with targets for each. The plan sets out the marketing funnel for email, from welcome to win-back, and the KPIs that will tell us whether it is working: revenue per send, conversion rate, list growth, return on ad spend (ROAS) where paid campaigns feed the list, lifetime value (LTV) of the customers email brings back, and cost per acquisition (CAC). You see the reasoning behind every campaign, including the ones we suggest you stop sending.

Build. We set up what the plan needs: the platform and its integrations, the segments and automations, the templates, the landing pages and the tracking in GA4, so email revenue can be connected to the rest of your marketing. If your team will run the programme, we hand over the setup with the documentation and training to use it.

Grow. We review performance every month against the agreed KPIs, and we test as we go: subject lines, send times, offers and creative, one change at a time. Flows that produce revenue get refined and extended; campaigns that do not are fixed or dropped. The programme grows with your list, and your list grows with the programme.

## Deliverables & reporting

You always know what you are getting. An email engagement delivers a written strategy, the audit findings behind it, a campaign calendar, the automated flows, the templates and the copy, and a reporting dashboard built around your KPIs. Campaigns are delivered to the agreed schedule, and every send is checked against the brief and tested before it goes out.

Reporting is monthly, in plain language. Each report shows revenue from email, leads, sales and cost per acquisition, with the contribution of each campaign and flow. We include what changed, what we did about it and what happens next. Your consultant walks you through the report, so the numbers are always explained by a person who knows your account.

We report on the customer journey, not just the last click. Attribution in GA4 shows how email works with search, paid media and social, so a campaign that starts the journey gets the credit it deserves. And because email runs on first-party data, the reporting improves as your list grows and your segments get sharper.

## Pricing & engagement models

The cost of email marketing depends on the size of your list, how often you send, and how much automation the programme needs. A simple monthly newsletter costs less than a full lifecycle programme with welcome, abandoned-cart, post-purchase and win-back flows. Platform and sending costs are separate from the agency fee, and we set those out clearly in the proposal.

We work in three ways. A monthly retainer covers the ongoing running of your programme: strategy, campaigns, flows, testing and reporting. A project engagement covers a defined piece of work, such as a platform migration, a flow build or a template set, with a fixed scope and a fixed end date. And a consulting engagement gives you the strategy and the setup, with your team doing the sending.

Most clients start with a retainer that covers the core flows and one or two campaigns a month, then add more as the list grows. That keeps the cost tied to the work, and the work tied to the revenue. Whichever model you choose, the proposal sets out the scope, the deliverables and the fee before you commit.
