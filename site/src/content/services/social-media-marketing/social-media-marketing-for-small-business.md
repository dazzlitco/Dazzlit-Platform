## Social Media Marketing for Small Business Services: what you get

Social media marketing is how a small business stays in front of the people most likely to buy from it. Your customers are already on Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, LinkedIn, and X — scrolling every day, asking friends for recommendations, and checking out businesses before they visit or call. If your business is not there, or if it is there but silent, those customers go to the competitor who is.

Our service covers the whole job: planning what to post, creating the posts, publishing them, replying to the people who respond, and reporting on what it all produced. That means a content calendar built around your offers and your seasons, short-form video and Reels, photos and graphics in your brand colours, captions written in your brand voice, and someone watching the inbox so no enquiry goes unanswered.

We work on the platforms that suit your business, not all of them for the sake of it. A restaurant needs Instagram and TikTok. A B2B supplier needs LinkedIn. A local service business needs Facebook and Google's local results as well. The plan picks the platforms where your buyers actually are, and puts the effort there.

The same approach works across sectors, with the content adjusted to each audience. A healthcare practice needs trust and clarity; a real estate agency needs photos, video, and speed. Our [healthcare social media marketing](/services/social-media-marketing/social-media-marketing-for-healthcare-and-medical-practices/) and [real estate social media marketing](/services/social-media-marketing/social-media-marketing-for-real-estate/) pages set out how the work changes for each. If you want the background first, our [social media marketing](/blog/social-media-marketing/) guide explains the channels, the formats, and how they fit together.

## Our Social Media Marketing for Small Business process

Every engagement follows the same five steps.

First, discovery. We learn how your business works, who your customers are, what they ask about, and what your competitors are posting. We look at your existing accounts to see what has worked, what has not, and what your audience responds to. We also set up social listening — watching what people say about your business and your market — so the content answers real questions instead of guessing at them.

Second, strategy. We agree the platforms, the content pillars, and the posting rhythm. A content pillar is a subject you return to again and again: your work, your customers, your expertise, and your offers. The calendar is built around those pillars, so the feed stays varied without losing its focus. We also agree the goal for each platform — enquiries, bookings, foot traffic, or sales — because the content has to serve a purpose.

Third, creation. Each week we produce the posts, the Reels and short-form video, the captions, and the hashtags. We use your photos and videos where we can, because real images of real work outperform stock photos. User-generated content — photos and reviews your customers post — is shared with permission, because it carries more trust than anything you say about yourself. Where it fits, we also work with local influencers and creators who already reach your audience, so your business is introduced by a voice they trust.

Fourth, publishing and community management. Posts go out at the times your audience is active, and someone replies to the comments and messages they generate. Community management is the part most small businesses drop first, and it is the part that produces customers: a question answered within the hour, a complaint handled gracefully in public, a compliment thanked properly. The algorithm also notices: accounts that reply get shown more than accounts that do not.

Fifth, measurement. We track reach, impressions, engagement rate, and — most importantly — the enquiries, bookings, and sales that came from social. Then we adjust the plan: more of what works, less of what does not.

## Deliverables & reporting

What you receive each month depends on the scope we agree, but it always includes the finished content, published on schedule, and a report that shows what it produced.

The content itself is planned in a shared calendar, so you always know what is going out and when. You can review anything before it is published, and you can ask for changes. The captions, hashtags, and images are all written and designed for you, so the only work on your side is approving the plan and sending us photos of new work when you have them.

The report covers the numbers that matter: enquiries, bookings, sales, and cost per acquisition. We also report reach (how many people saw your content), impressions (how often it was seen), and engagement rate (how many people interacted with it), because those numbers show whether the content is being seen and whether people care about it. But they are supporting detail. The headline is always business: did social media bring in customers this month, and what did each one cost? The report also notes which posts earned the most reach and which ones earned the most replies, so the next month's calendar leans into what your audience actually responds to. And because the work is shared across your team, the calendar is visible to everyone — no surprises, and no post going out that you have not seen.

## Pricing & engagement models

The cost of social media marketing for a small business depends on what is involved. The main factors are how many platforms you are on, how often you post, and how much of the work we take on. A business on two platforms with a few posts a week costs less than a business on five platforms with daily video and full community management. Video takes more time than static posts, and replying to every comment takes more time than posting alone.

We work in two ways. A monthly retainer suits businesses that want social media handled properly, week in and week out — planning, a content calendar, creation, posting, replies, and a monthly report. A project engagement suits a defined job with a clear end, such as launching a new account, a campaign around a seasonal offer, or a set of videos for a launch. Either way, your proposal sets out the scope, the deliverables, and the fee before any work starts, so there are no surprises.
