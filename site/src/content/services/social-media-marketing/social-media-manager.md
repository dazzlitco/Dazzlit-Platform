## Social Media Manager Services: what you get

A social media manager is the person who runs your day-to-day presence on social platforms — and the job is bigger than posting. A manager plans what your business will say, produces the content, publishes it, answers the comments and messages it generates, and tracks whether any of it led to customers. Posting is only one part of the job, and on its own it is the least valuable part.

Our [social media management](/services/social-media-marketing/) service gives you a dedicated manager backed by a team. The manager owns your accounts, your content calendar, and your reporting. Behind them sit the specialists they call on: designers, video editors, and the paid media team who can put your best posts in front of more of the right people. That structure matters, because one person rarely does all of it well — strategy, production, community, and measurement are four different jobs, and when one of them is missing, the whole programme suffers.

Your manager works on the platforms that suit your business: Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, LinkedIn, and X. They plan the content, write the captions, design or edit the posts, publish on a steady schedule, reply to comments and direct messages, and report on what it all produced. You get a feed that stays active and on-brand, an inbox that never sits unanswered, and a monthly report that shows what the work did for your business.

Social analytics sit behind all of it. Your manager watches which posts earn reach and which ones earn replies, and uses that evidence to decide what to post next. An account that keeps doing what worked last month, without checking whether it still works, drifts. Ours is reviewed every month, so the plan changes when the evidence changes.

## Our Social Media Manager process

Every engagement follows the same five steps.

First, discovery. Your manager learns how your business works, who your customers are, what they ask about, and what your competitors are posting. We look at your existing accounts to see what has worked and what has not. We also set up social listening — watching what people say about your business and your market — so the content answers real questions instead of guessing at them.

Second, strategy. We agree the platforms, the goals, and the content pillars: the subjects you return to again and again, such as your work, your customers, your expertise, and your offers. The manager builds a content calendar around those pillars, so the feed stays varied without losing focus. We also agree what success looks like — enquiries, bookings, foot traffic, or sales — because the content has to serve a purpose.

Third, production. Each week your manager produces the posts: Reels and short-form video, photos and graphics in your brand colours, captions written in your brand voice, and hashtags chosen for reach. We use your photos and videos where we can, because real images of real work outperform stock photos. User-generated content — photos and reviews your customers post — is shared with permission, because it carries more trust than anything you say about yourself.

Fourth, publishing and community. Posts go out at the times your audience is active, and your manager replies to the comments and messages they generate. Community management is the part that gets dropped first when someone is stretched thin, and it is the part that produces customers: a question answered within the hour, a complaint handled gracefully in public, a compliment thanked properly. The algorithm notices too — accounts that reply get shown more than accounts that do not.

Fifth, measurement. Your manager tracks reach, impressions, engagement rate, and the enquiries, bookings, and sales that came from social, then adjusts the plan. Measurement is what separates a manager from a poster: followers and likes are easy to report and mean almost nothing on their own. What matters is whether the work brought in business.

## Deliverables & reporting

What you receive each month depends on the scope we agree, but it always includes the finished content, published on schedule, and a report that shows what it produced.

The content itself is planned in a shared calendar, so you always know what is going out and when. You can review anything before it is published, and you can ask for changes. Your manager also keeps the inbox covered, so comments and messages get real replies in your brand voice — and anything that needs your team, such as a sales enquiry or a complaint, is passed to you quickly.

The report covers the numbers that matter: enquiries, bookings, sales, and cost per acquisition. We also report reach (how many people saw your content), impressions (how often it was seen), and engagement rate (how many people interacted with it), because those numbers show whether the content is being seen and whether people care about it. But they are supporting detail. The headline is always business: did social media bring in customers this month, and what did each one cost?

## Pricing & engagement models

The cost of a social media manager depends on what is involved. The main factors are how many platforms your manager covers, how often you post, and how much of the job we take on. A business on two platforms with a few posts a week costs less than a business on five platforms with daily video and full community management. Video takes more time than static posts, and replying to every comment takes more time than posting alone.

We work in two ways. A monthly retainer suits businesses that want their social media handled properly, week in and week out — a dedicated manager, a content calendar, creation, posting, replies, and a monthly report. A project engagement suits a defined job with a clear end, such as launching a new account, a campaign around a seasonal offer, or a set of videos for a launch. Either way, your proposal sets out the scope, the deliverables, and the fee before any work starts, so there are no surprises.
