## WordPress SEO Services: what you get

WordPress powers a large share of the web, and it is a good platform for SEO — but only when it is set up properly. Out of the box, WordPress gives you the basics: clean URLs, a sitemap, and an easy way to publish content. What it does not give you is a site that ranks on its own. Too many plugins slow it down, the wrong settings create duplicate pages, and content published without a plan does not rank for anything. Our WordPress SEO service fixes the setup and then does the work that makes the site rank.

A WordPress SEO programme from Dazzlit starts with a full audit of your site: the theme, the plugins, the settings, the speed, and the content. We fix the technical problems — duplicate content from tags and archives, slow loading, broken links, missing sitemaps, and schema markup that is absent or wrong — and we set up the SEO plugin properly, so every page has the titles, descriptions, and structured data it needs. Then we do the ongoing work: keyword research, content, internal linking, and link building, with monthly reporting on the numbers that matter.

Much of the work is in the setup. WordPress makes it easy to add plugins, and that is part of the problem: a site with thirty plugins is usually slower than a site with ten, and two SEO plugins fighting each other can do more harm than none at all. We review every plugin that touches search, speed, or structure, remove the ones that hurt, and configure the ones that help — including the SEO plugin itself, where the wrong settings can create duplicate titles, unwanted archive pages, and sitemaps that list pages Google should never see. Our article on [plugins for SEO in WordPress](/blog/plugin-for-seo-in-wordpress/) covers the choices in more detail.

This service suits any business running on WordPress, from a small brochure site to a large blog or store. If your site is slow, if your pages do not rank, or if you have been publishing content that brings in no traffic, the answer is usually in the setup — and that is what we fix first. Our [WordPress SEO guide](/resources/guides/wordpress-seo/) explains the foundations in more detail.

## Our WordPress SEO Services process

Every engagement follows the same process, so you always know what is happening and why.

1. **Audit.** We review your website, your rankings, and your competitors. We look at the theme, the plugins, the settings, the speed, and the content, and we check your site in Google Search Console to see how Google views it. We agree on the targets that matter — calls, form fills, and sales, not just traffic.
2. **Keyword research.** We build a keyword map: the searches your customers use, grouped by service and topic, with keyword difficulty assessed so we know what you can win and in what order.
3. **Technical fixes.** We fix the issues the audit found — duplicate content, speed, mobile usability, broken links, and the SEO plugin settings — so the rest of the work has a solid base. We remove or replace the plugins that hurt performance, and we set up caching and image optimisation properly.
4. **On-page and content.** We optimise your service pages and posts, then publish new content where there are gaps: service pages, guides, and answers to the questions your customers ask. Content is planned around the keyword map, not around a publishing quota.
5. **Internal linking.** We connect related pages so both Google and your visitors can follow the thread — and so the authority your best pages earn flows to the pages that need it.
6. **Authority.** We earn links and mentions from relevant sources, steadily, rather than buying links in bulk.
7. **Report and refine.** Every month we report on rankings, traffic, and leads, and we adjust the plan based on what the data shows.

The first month is mostly audit and fixes. Content and link work builds from the second month on, and the plan is reviewed with you each month.

## Deliverables & reporting

You will always know what you paid for and what it produced. Each month you receive a report that shows your rankings for the keywords that matter, your organic traffic, and — most importantly — the leads that came from search: calls, form submissions, and direction requests. We report on leads, sales, and revenue, not vanity numbers, and we explain what changed and why in plain language.

The deliverables behind the report are yours to keep. They include the initial technical audit with every issue found and fixed, the keyword map for your site, the content we publish, and a record of the links earned. If you ever need to show someone what was done, or move the work in-house, nothing is hidden from you. That includes the plugin and settings changes: we document what was changed and why, so your developers can see exactly what was done.

WordPress reporting also covers the health of the site itself. Plugin updates, theme changes, and new content can all affect rankings, so the monthly report notes what changed on the site and whether it helped or hurt. If an update breaks something — a sitemap that stops working, a page that disappears from the index — we catch it in the monthly checks and fix it before it costs you traffic.

Reporting also covers the channels that support SEO. If you run paid search or social media with us, the same report shows what each channel contributed, so you can see how search fits into the whole picture.

## Results / case study

WordPress SEO takes time, and honest reporting means saying so. Rankings do not change overnight, and anyone who promises instant results is selling something Google will eventually penalise. What we can show you is what steady, well-planned SEO does over months — and WordPress sites often move faster at first, because many of the early fixes are simple changes to settings and plugins.

In one SEO engagement, we helped a local service business improve its Google rankings, attract qualified visitors, and generate more organic leads. Organic traffic rose by 215% in six months, and leads grew 3.2 times over the same period. That is the shape of a good result: not a one-week spike, but a business that keeps being found by more of the right people. You can read the full story on our [case studies](/case-studies/) page.

## Pricing & engagement models

There is no single price for WordPress SEO, because no two WordPress sites need the same work. What you pay depends on the size of your site, how many pages and posts you have, how competitive your market is, and how much content and authority building is needed. A small brochure site needs a smaller programme than a large blog or store with hundreds of pages.

Most of our WordPress clients work on a monthly retainer: a fixed scope of work each month — technical monitoring, content, and link building — reviewed and adjusted monthly. One-off projects, such as a full audit, a plugin and settings clean-up, or a migration to a new theme, are quoted separately. Every proposal lists the scope, the deliverables, and the monthly work before you commit, so you can compare it with any other quote on the basis of what is actually included.
