## B2B SEO Agency: what you get

Business-to-business SEO is different from consumer SEO, and the difference is the buyer. A B2B buyer does not impulse-buy. They research a problem for weeks or months, read comparison guides, check vendors against a shortlist, and get approval from colleagues before anyone signs anything. Often several people are involved, each searching for something different: one looks for the problem, another for the solution, another for proof it works. Our job is to make sure your business is found and trusted at every step of that journey.

A B2B SEO programme from Dazzlit covers the full buying journey. We research the keywords your buyers use at each stage — from the first problem search to the final vendor comparison — and build the pages that answer them: service pages, solution pages, comparison pages, guides, and case studies. We fix the technical foundations so Google can crawl and index a large site properly. We build topical authority in your niche, so your site is the one buyers keep coming back to. And we earn links and mentions from the industry sources your buyers already trust.

This is not local SEO with bigger words. It is a content and authority programme built for long sales cycles, committee decisions, and high-value deals, where one good lead can be worth a year of consumer traffic. If your buyers are consumers rather than businesses, our [small business SEO services](/services/seo/small-business-seo-services/) are built for local search instead. And if you want to see how the work is priced, read our [SEO pricing](/services/seo/seo-pricing-and-packages/) page.

## B2B Search Engine Optimization Agency

Search engines are where B2B research starts. Before a buyer talks to sales, they search: for the problem, for the category, for vendors, for pricing, for proof. The pages that answer those searches shape the shortlist, often before your sales team knows the buyer exists. That is why B2B SEO is a sales activity, not just a marketing one — it puts your business in front of buyers at the moment they are forming opinions.

The work starts with search intent. B2B searches fall into clear groups: informational searches from people learning about a problem, comparison searches from people weighing options, and transactional searches from people ready to talk. Each group needs different content, and each page must match what the searcher actually wants. A guide that explains a problem should not read like a sales page, and a pricing page should not hide behind a form. We map keywords to pages by intent, so every important search has a page built for it.

Then comes the technical work. B2B sites are often large, with hundreds of pages, multiple products, and content in several languages or for several markets. We make sure Google can crawl and index that structure cleanly: fixing duplicate pages, broken links, slow templates, and pages that are accidentally blocked. We check the site in Google Search Console and fix what it reports. We add schema markup so Google understands your business, your products, your reviews, and your content, and we build internal linking that connects related topics so both Google and your buyers can follow the thread.

Authority is the third part. Google looks for E-E-A-T — experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness — and in B2B that means showing real expertise: content written by people who know the subject, evidence from real work, and backlinks from respected industry sources. We also watch algorithm updates and the growth of AI Overviews, the AI-generated answers that now sit above the ordinary results, so your content is written to be quoted and cited, not just ranked. Our [SEO guide](/resources/guides/seo/) explains these foundations in more detail.

## Our B2B SEO Agency process

Every engagement follows the same process, so you always know what is happening and why.

1. **Audit.** We review your website, your rankings, and your competitors, and we interview your sales team about how deals actually happen — what buyers ask, what objections come up, and which pages help close. The audit covers technical health, content gaps, and authority.
2. **Keyword research.** We build a keyword map by buying stage: problem searches, solution searches, comparison searches, and vendor searches, with keyword difficulty assessed for each so we know what you can win and in what order.
3. **Technical fixes.** We fix the issues the audit found — crawl and indexing problems, speed, duplicate content, and site structure — so the rest of the work has a solid base.
4. **Content.** We build or improve the pages the keyword map calls for: service and solution pages, comparison pages, guides, and proof pages such as case studies. Content is planned around the buying journey, not around a publishing quota.
5. **Authority.** We earn links and mentions from industry publications, partners, and associations, and we make sure your best content is cited where your buyers look.
6. **Report and refine.** Every month we report on rankings, traffic, qualified leads, and pipeline, and we adjust the plan based on what the data shows.

The first month is mostly audit and fixes. Content and authority work builds from the second month on, and the plan is reviewed with you each month.

## Deliverables & reporting

B2B reporting has to connect to sales, or it is useless. Each month you receive a report that shows your rankings for the keywords that matter, your organic traffic, and — most importantly — the qualified leads that came from search: demo requests, contact forms, downloads, and the pipeline they produced. We report on leads, pipeline, revenue, and cost per acquisition, not vanity numbers, and we explain what changed and why in plain language.

The deliverables behind the report are yours to keep: the technical audit, the keyword map by buying stage, the content we publish, and a record of the links earned. If your sales team wants to know which pages help close deals, the report shows that too. For a closer look at how we plan search work, read our [SEO strategy](/blog/seo-strategy/) article.

## Results / case study

B2B SEO takes time, and the results take longer to show in revenue because buying decisions take longer. That does not mean the work is slow to start working: rankings and qualified traffic usually move first, and the pipeline follows. The right way to judge B2B SEO is by the quality of the leads, not the volume of the traffic.

In one SEO engagement, we helped a local service business improve its Google rankings, attract qualified visitors, and generate more organic leads. Organic traffic rose by 215% in six months, and leads grew 3.2 times over the same period. The pattern is the same in B2B, even if the numbers move more slowly: steady, well-planned work compounds, and the pages built in the first months keep bringing in buyers later. ## Pricing & engagement models

There is no single price for B2B SEO, because no two B2B businesses need the same work. What you pay depends on the size of your site, how many products or markets you cover, how competitive your niche is, and how much content and authority building is needed. A company selling one product in one market needs a smaller programme than a company with several products in several countries.

Most of our B2B clients work on a monthly retainer: a fixed scope of work each month — technical monitoring, content, and link building — reviewed and adjusted monthly. One-off projects, such as a full audit, a site migration, or a content programme for a new product launch, are quoted separately. Every proposal lists the scope, the deliverables, and the monthly work before you commit, so you can compare it with any other quote on the basis of what is actually included.
