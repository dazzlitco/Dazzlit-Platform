## eCommerce SEO Services: what you get

eCommerce SEO is the work of making your online store easy to find when shoppers search for what you sell. When someone searches for a product by name, by type, or by the problem it solves, the stores that show up first get the sale. Our job is to put your store in that group — on the product pages, the category pages, and the guides that shoppers read before they buy.

An eCommerce SEO programme from Dazzlit covers the whole store. We start with a technical audit, because online stores have problems other sites do not: thousands of product pages, filters that create endless duplicate URLs, out-of-stock products, and slow category pages. Then we optimise your category and product pages, fix the structure so Google can crawl the catalogue cleanly, and build the content that brings in shoppers who are still researching. We add schema markup so your products can show prices, availability, and review stars in the search results. And we build your store's authority with links from relevant sources.

This works for stores on any platform — Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, and custom builds — and for catalogues of any size. What matters is not the platform but the structure: a store that Google can understand, with pages that match what shoppers actually search for. Our [eCommerce SEO guide](/resources/guides/ecommerce-seo/) explains the foundations in more detail.

## WooCommerce SEO

WooCommerce stores run on WordPress, which gives you control — and gives you plenty of ways to get SEO wrong. Common problems include duplicate content from product variations and filters, thin product pages copied from suppliers, slow loading from too many plugins, and category pages that Google cannot make sense of.

Our WooCommerce SEO work starts with the structure. We make sure your categories match the way shoppers search, that each product has a unique page worth ranking, and that filters and variations do not create thousands of duplicate URLs. We optimise product titles, descriptions, and images, and we add schema markup for products, prices, availability, and reviews. We also check the plugins that affect SEO — caching, sitemaps, redirects — and remove or replace the ones that hurt performance.

Because WooCommerce is WordPress, the content side is strong: you can publish buying guides, comparisons, and answers to the questions your shoppers ask, all on the same site as your store. We build that content around the searches that lead to sales, and we link it to the products it supports, so a shopper who reads a guide is one click from buying. Our [WordPress SEO services](/services/seo/wordpress-seo-services/) cover the platform side in more detail.

## Shopify SEO Agency

Shopify is built for selling, but out of the box it is not built for ranking. Common problems include duplicate content from product URLs, limited control over the site structure, collection pages that compete with each other, and apps that slow the store down. Our Shopify SEO work fixes those problems and builds on what Shopify does well.

We start with the structure: collections organised around the way shoppers search, product pages with unique titles and descriptions, and filters that do not create duplicate URLs. We optimise your theme for speed and mobile usability, because most shoppers arrive on a phone, and we check your Core Web Vitals — the measurements of how fast your pages load and how stable they feel. We add schema markup so your products can show prices, availability, and review stars in the search results.

Then we build the content that brings in shoppers before they are ready to buy: buying guides, comparisons, and answers to the questions your customers ask. That content is linked to the products it supports, so a reader becomes a buyer. Our [Shopify SEO guide](/resources/guides/shopify-seo/) explains the platform's particular issues in more detail, and our [best eCommerce SEO](/blog/best-ecommerce-seo/) article covers what works across platforms.

Our Shopify SEO services cover the full job, from the technical base to the content that brings in sales.

## Our eCommerce SEO Services process

Every engagement follows the same process, so you always know what is happening and why.

1. **Audit.** We review your store, your rankings, and your competitors. We look at technical health, catalogue structure, content gaps, and backlinks, and we agree on the targets that matter — sales and revenue, not just traffic.
2. **Keyword research.** We build a keyword map by intent: product searches from shoppers ready to buy, category searches from shoppers comparing options, and informational searches from shoppers still researching. We assess keyword difficulty for each, so we know what you can win and in what order.
3. **Technical fixes.** We fix the issues the audit found — crawl and indexing problems, duplicate URLs, speed, and mobile usability — so the rest of the work has a solid base.
4. **On-page optimisation.** We optimise your category and product pages: titles, descriptions, images, and schema markup for prices, availability, and reviews.
5. **Content.** We publish buying guides, comparisons, and answers that attract shoppers before they buy, and link them to the products they support.
6. **Authority.** We earn links and mentions from relevant sources, steadily, rather than buying links in bulk.
7. **Report and refine.** Every month we report on rankings, traffic, organic sales, and revenue, and we adjust the plan based on what the data shows.

The first month is mostly audit and fixes. Content and link work builds from the second month on, and the plan is reviewed with you each month.

## Pricing & engagement models

There is no single price for eCommerce SEO, because no two stores need the same work. What you pay depends on the size of your catalogue, how many categories and products you have, how competitive your market is, and how much content and authority building is needed. A small store with a few dozen products needs a smaller programme than a catalogue with thousands of products competing nationally.

Most of our eCommerce clients work on a monthly retainer: a fixed scope of work each month — technical monitoring, content, and link building — reviewed and adjusted monthly. One-off projects, such as a full audit, a platform migration, or a rebuild of the catalogue structure, are quoted separately. Every proposal lists the scope, the deliverables, and the monthly work before you commit, so you can compare it with any other quote on the basis of what is actually included.
