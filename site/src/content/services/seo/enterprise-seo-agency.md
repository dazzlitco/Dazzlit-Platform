## Enterprise SEO Agency: what you get

Enterprise SEO is SEO at scale: large websites, many products, several markets, and teams of people who all need to agree. A site with thousands of pages, content in more than one language, and a history of redesigns and migrations does not behave like a small business site, and it cannot be managed like one. Our enterprise SEO service is built for that scale — the technical complexity, the coordination, and the reporting that senior stakeholders expect.

An enterprise programme from Dazzlit covers the whole site. We audit and fix the technical foundations: crawling, indexing, site structure, speed, and duplicate content across thousands of pages. We build keyword and content plans by product, market, and buying stage. We manage authority at scale, earning links and mentions that move a large site. And we run the programme with the governance an enterprise needs: clear priorities, documented decisions, and reporting that speaks to marketing, sales, and leadership in their own terms.

This is not small business SEO with a bigger budget. It is a different job, with different risks: a single technical mistake can affect thousands of pages, and a single decision can take weeks to approve. We have built our enterprise service around those realities. If your site is smaller and simpler, our [SEO services](/services/seo/) cover the same work at a smaller scale.

## Enterprise SEO Firms

Choosing an enterprise SEO firm is a bigger decision than choosing a small business agency, because the stakes are higher and the switching costs are real. The wrong firm can waste a year and damage a site that took years to build. So what should you look for?

First, look for experience with sites your size. Ask how many pages the firm has managed, how many markets, and how they handle technical work at scale. A firm that has only worked on small sites will not know what breaks when a site has ten thousand pages. Second, look for process: how they audit, how they prioritise, how they document decisions, and how they report. Enterprise work without process becomes chaos. Third, look for honesty about timelines. Enterprise SEO takes months to show results, and any firm that promises otherwise is selling you a story.

Fourth, look at how they work with your teams. Enterprise SEO touches developers, content writers, product managers, and legal reviewers. The firm has to work inside your processes, not around them, and it has to communicate in a way that each team understands. Finally, look at reporting: can they show the work in terms your leadership cares about — pipeline, revenue, and return — or only in rankings and traffic? Our [case studies](/case-studies/) page shows what steady, well-planned SEO produces over months.

## Enterprise SEO Solutions

Enterprise SEO solutions are the pieces of a large programme, put together to fit your site and your organisation.

Technical SEO at scale means managing crawling and indexing across thousands of pages, keeping the site fast, fixing duplicate content, and making sure the site structure makes sense to Google and to your visitors. It also means managing the risks that come with scale: migrations, redesigns, platform changes, and international rollouts, where one mistake can affect a whole market.

Content at scale means planning and producing content by product, market, and buying stage, with a keyword map that covers the whole site and a publishing process that your teams can actually follow. It means building topical authority in each of your niches, so your site is the one buyers keep coming back to, and it means keeping content fresh as products and markets change.

Authority at scale means earning links and mentions from the sources that matter in your industry, steadily, and managing your brand's reputation in search results across markets. It also means watching algorithm updates and the growth of AI Overviews, the AI-generated answers that now sit above the ordinary results, so your content is written to be quoted and cited, not just ranked.

And governance ties it together: priorities agreed with your teams, decisions documented, and reporting that shows each stakeholder what they need. If you want to estimate the return before you start, try our [enterprise SEO ROI calculator](/resources/tools/enterprise-seo-roi-calculator/).

## Enterprise SEO Consulting

Enterprise SEO consulting is expert advice for businesses that already have a team — in-house marketers, developers, or another agency — and need a specialist to guide them. Instead of doing the work for you, we tell you what to do, in what order, and why, and we review the work as it is done.

Consulting usually starts with an audit, then a plan: the fixes ranked by impact, with clear instructions your teams can follow. We stay involved while the work is done, answering questions, checking the output, and flagging anything that could cause problems. Common consulting jobs include site migrations, platform changes, redesigns, international rollouts, and recovering from a drop in rankings after an algorithm update.

This model works well when the problem is knowledge, not capacity: your teams can do the work, but they need someone who has done it at scale before to steer them. It also works well for one-off decisions, such as whether to restructure a large site or enter a new market. ## Our Enterprise SEO Agency process

Every engagement follows the same process, so you always know what is happening and why.

1. **Audit.** We review your website, your rankings, and your competitors, and we talk to your teams about how the site is managed. The audit covers technical health, content gaps, and authority, at the scale of your whole site.
2. **Strategy.** We agree on priorities with your stakeholders: which products, which markets, and which buying stages matter most, and what success looks like in terms of pipeline and revenue.
3. **Technical fixes.** We fix the issues the audit found — crawl and indexing problems, speed, duplicate content, and site structure — working with your developers and following your release processes.
4. **Content.** We build or improve the pages the strategy calls for, by product, market, and buying stage, with a publishing process your teams can follow.
5. **Authority.** We earn links and mentions from industry sources, and we manage your brand's visibility in search results across markets.
6. **Report and refine.** Every month we report on rankings, traffic, qualified leads, and pipeline, and we adjust the plan based on what the data shows.

The first months are mostly audit, strategy, and fixes. Content and authority work builds from there, and the plan is reviewed with your stakeholders regularly.

## Deliverables & reporting

Enterprise reporting has to work for several audiences at once. Each month you receive a report that shows your rankings for the keywords that matter, your organic traffic, and the qualified leads and pipeline that came from search. We report on leads, pipeline, revenue, and cost per acquisition, not vanity numbers, and we explain what changed and why in plain language.

The report is built in layers: a summary for leadership, detail for marketing, and technical detail for developers. The deliverables behind it are yours to keep: the audit, the keyword map, the content published, and a record of the links earned. Nothing is hidden, and nothing is held back if you decide to move the work in-house.

## Pricing & engagement models

There is no single price for enterprise SEO, because no two enterprise businesses need the same work. What you pay depends on the size of your site, how many products and markets you cover, how competitive your niche is, and how much content and authority building is needed. A company with one product in one market needs a smaller programme than a company with several products in several countries.

Most of our enterprise clients work on a monthly retainer: a fixed scope of work each month — technical monitoring, content, and link building — reviewed and adjusted monthly. One-off projects, such as a full audit, a site migration, or a programme for a new market launch, are quoted separately. Every proposal lists the scope, the deliverables, and the monthly work before you commit, so you can compare it with any other quote on the basis of what is actually included.
