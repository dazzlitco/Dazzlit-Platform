## Google Review Link Services: what you get

A Google review link is the shortest path between a happy customer and a public review. Instead of asking a customer to find your business, open your profile and work out where to click, you send them one link — or one QR code — that takes them straight to the review form. Our Google review link service builds that path into a system that asks every customer, at the right moment, in a way that actually gets answered.

Here is what the service covers.

- **Your review link, set up properly.** We create and check your Google review link for every location, so the link takes customers to the right profile and the right form. For multi-location businesses, each branch gets its own link.
- **QR codes and short links.** We produce QR codes for receipts, packaging, counters and vehicles, and short links for email, SMS and invoices, so asking for a review fits the way you already talk to customers. Our free [Google review QR code](/resources/tools/google-review-qr-code/) tool makes a code in a minute; we build the full system around it.
- **Review requests.** We set up the requests themselves — the timing, the wording and the channel — so they go out after the job is done, while the experience is fresh. Requests are short, polite and easy to answer, because a request that feels like a chore gets ignored.
- **Responses and monitoring.** We monitor new reviews, draft responses for you to approve, and flag the negative ones that need a fast, careful reply. A thoughtful response to a bad review often matters more than the review itself.
- **Reporting.** We track how many reviews were requested and received, your average star rating, review velocity and the enquiries coming from your profile, and report on it every month.

The whole point is to make asking routine. Businesses that ask every customer get reviews steadily; businesses that ask when they remember get reviews in bursts, then nothing. A steady flow of recent reviews is what customers trust and what Google rewards.

## Google Maps Reviews

Most local customers meet your business on Google Maps first. They search for what they need, compare the businesses nearby, and judge them on three things: how close they are, how many stars they have, and how recent the reviews are. A business with a 4.8-star rating and reviews from this week beats a business with a 4.9-star rating and reviews from two years ago.

That is why Google Maps reviews deserve their own attention. We make sure your Google Business Profile is complete and accurate — hours, services, photos, categories — because a profile with missing information ranks lower and converts worse. We then build the review flow that keeps fresh reviews arriving, and we watch how the profile performs in the map results: views, calls, direction requests and website clicks.

It also helps to check how your business currently appears in search before you start asking. Our [Google ad preview tool](/blog/google-ad-preview-tool/) shows you the ad as a searcher would see it, which is a useful check on whether the profile and the ads are telling the same story. If they are not, we fix the mismatch first — a customer who clicks an ad and lands on a profile that contradicts it rarely converts.

## Google Reviews for Business

Reviews are social proof, and for a local business they are often the deciding factor. A customer choosing between two plumbers, two dentists or two restaurants will read the reviews before they call, and they will trust the pattern they see there more than anything you say about yourself.

Our work for businesses starts with the ask. We write the request messages, choose the timing — usually within a day of the job, while the experience is fresh — and set up the follow-up for customers who do not answer the first time. We also set up the internal habit: your team knows when and how to ask, and the system reminds them when they forget.

Then we handle what comes back. Positive reviews get a thank-you and, where appropriate, a place on your website as testimonials. Negative reviews get a calm, professional response that addresses the issue and invites the customer to talk. And every review is checked against the rules: we never offer incentives for reviews, and we never write or buy fake ones. Google's policies prohibit both, and the damage when they are caught — removed reviews, a suspended profile, lost trust — is far worse than the short-term gain.

For the full picture on how reviews work for a business, our guide to [Google business reviews](/resources/guides/google-business-reviews/) covers the setup, the ask and the response.

## Google Map Reviews

Google Maps reviews and Google Search reviews are the same reviews, shown in two places — but customers read them differently. On Maps, they are comparing you with the businesses around you, side by side, on stars and recency. In Search, they are deciding whether to click through to you at all.

That is why managing Google reviews means managing both views. We keep the profile accurate and the reviews flowing, and we watch the metrics that show whether the map listing is earning its keep: how often it appears, how often it is clicked, and how many of those clicks become calls or visits.

Review velocity matters here. A steady stream of new reviews tells Google the business is active and current, and it tells customers the rating is real. We aim for a consistent pace rather than a spike: a sudden flood of reviews looks suspicious and often triggers a filter, while a slow, steady flow builds the rating month by month. We also make sure reviews are spread across your locations rather than concentrated in one branch, so no location is left with an empty profile.

## Our Google Review Link process

Every engagement starts with an audit of where you stand: your profile, your current reviews, your star rating, your review velocity, and how you compare with the competitors around you. We also check whether you already have a review link and whether it actually works — a surprising number do not.

From there, the work follows a fixed rhythm.

**1. Set up.** We create and test the review link and QR code for each location, fix the profile details, and confirm the business information is consistent everywhere it appears.

**2. Build the ask.** We write the request messages and the templates your team will use, choose the timing and the channel — email, SMS, receipt or in person — and agree the follow-up for customers who do not respond. Our [review request template](/resources/templates/review-request-template/) gives you the wording we start from.

**3. Launch.** Requests go out to recent customers first, so the first new reviews arrive quickly and the system is proven before it scales to every customer.

**4. Monitor and respond.** We watch for new reviews daily, draft responses, and flag anything that needs your attention — especially negative reviews, where a fast, honest reply matters most.

**5. Report and improve.** Each month we review the numbers — requests sent, reviews received, star rating, velocity, responses — and adjust the ask: the timing, the wording, the channel. Small changes to the request often make a large difference to the response rate.
