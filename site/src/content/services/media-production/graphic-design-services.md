## Graphic Design Services: what you get

Good design is not decoration. It is how a customer decides, in a few seconds, whether your business is worth their attention — on your website, in an ad, in a sales deck or on a product page. Our graphic design services give you that judgement on tap: senior designers who know your brand, work to a brief, and deliver files your team can actually use.

Here is what the service covers.

- **Brand design.** Logos, color palettes, typography and the rules for using them, so every piece of marketing looks like it came from the same company. If your brand needs a refresh rather than a rebuild, we start from what already works.
- **Marketing design.** Ads, social templates, brochures, presentations, infographics and trade show graphics — designed to a brief, delivered on time, and built around the message the piece has to carry.
- **Digital design.** Website layouts, landing pages, email templates and app screens, designed with the developers who build them, so what is approved is what ships.
- **Design for video.** Storyboards, title cards, thumbnails, lower thirds and motion graphics, so your video work and your static design speak the same visual language.
- **Production files.** Every deliverable comes with the source files and the export formats you need — print, web and social — in the right aspect ratios, so nothing has to be rebuilt by your team.

Design sits inside our wider [video production](/services/media-production/) work, which means the same team can take a concept from a storyboard through the shoot, editing and distribution. That matters when a campaign needs both a film and the ads, social posts and landing page that go with it.

## Branding Agency

A brand is more than a logo. It is the set of choices — name, look, voice, values — that make a customer remember you and choose you over the next option. As a branding agency, we work from strategy first: who you serve, what you offer that others do not, and how you want to be seen.

The work usually starts with discovery: interviews with your team, a look at your competitors, and an honest review of the brand you have now. From there we build the identity — logo, typography, color, imagery and tone — and the guidelines that keep it consistent when different people produce work. The guidelines matter as much as the logo: a brand that looks different on every channel is not a brand, it is a collection of files.

If you are a B2B business with a longer sales cycle, our [B2B branding agency](/services/media-production/b2b-branding-agency/) work follows the same process, weighted toward the materials your sales team actually uses — decks, proposals, one-pagers and event graphics.

## Brand Marketing Agency

Branding sets the direction; brand marketing puts it to work. As a brand marketing agency, we plan the campaigns and content that carry the brand into the market — launches, seasonal pushes, thought leadership and the always-on presence that keeps you visible between campaigns.

The difference between brand marketing and a one-off design job is the through-line. Every ad, post and page should build the same associations in the customer's mind, month after month. We plan that through-line with you, then design and produce the assets it needs. That includes the unglamorous work that makes campaigns land: templates your team can reuse, a library of approved imagery, and clear rules for what good looks like.

Brand marketing is measured differently from direct response. We track awareness, recall and engagement alongside leads, and we are honest about which numbers move quickly and which take time.

## Creative Marketing Agency

A creative marketing agency earns attention rather than buying it. That means ideas strong enough to be shared, remembered and talked about — and disciplined enough to connect back to the sale.

Our creative work starts with the audience, not the format. What do they care about, what are they tired of seeing, and what would make them stop scrolling? From there we develop concepts, test the strongest ones, and produce them across the formats the campaign needs: film, animation, motion graphics, social and print. User-generated content (UGC) has a part to play here too — real customers, filmed well, often outperform polished brand films for trust, and we plan for both.

Creativity without craft is just noise. Every concept is produced to a standard your brand can stand behind, in the formats and aspect ratios each channel demands, and with the distribution planned before the shoot, not after.

## Our Graphic Design Services process

Design projects fail in predictable ways: vague briefs, endless revisions, files that do not work. Our process is built to avoid them.

**1. Brief.** We agree what the design has to do, who it is for, where it will appear, and when it is needed. A good brief answers those four questions; if it does not, we ask until it does.

**2. Concepts.** For brand and campaign work, we present directions rather than finished pieces — two or three routes, each with a reason. You choose a direction, and we develop it. For production design, we confirm the format, the content and the deadline, then get on with it.

**3. Design and review.** Work is shared in rounds, with clear feedback at each stage. We ask for decisions, not open-ended opinions, and we keep a record of what was agreed so nothing is revisited later.

**4. Production.** Approved designs are finished to print or screen standard, in every format and aspect ratio the brief calls for. For video work, this is where pre-production, scripting and the storyboard meet the shoot: the edit, the motion graphics, the animation, the color grading and the sound design are all planned against the same storyboard, so the finished film matches the design that was approved.

**5. Delivery and distribution.** You receive the source files and the exports, organised and labelled, with a short note on how to use them. For video, delivery includes the versions each channel needs and the basics of video SEO — titles, descriptions and thumbnails — so the work can be found as well as watched.

## Deliverables & reporting

Deliverables depend on the brief, but a typical engagement includes the finished designs, the source files, the export formats, and — for brand work — the brand guidelines. For video projects, deliverables include the finished film in each required format, the storyboard and script, and the supporting graphics such as thumbnails and title cards.

Reporting matches the work. Design projects are reported against the brief: what was delivered, when, and whether it met the standard agreed at the start. Campaigns and video are reported on performance: views and watch time, engagement, clicks through to your site, and the leads or sales the work contributed to. Where video is distributed across channels, the report shows which versions and placements performed best, so the next round of creative starts from evidence rather than opinion.

## Pricing & engagement models

Design work is priced by scope, and scope depends on volume, complexity and turnaround. A monthly retainer suits teams that need a steady flow of design — ads, social, decks, sales materials — and want a designer who knows the brand without a new brief each time. A fixed-scope project suits a defined job, such as a brand refresh, a launch campaign or a video, where the deliverables and the deadline are agreed in advance.

What affects the price is the amount of design, the number of formats, the rounds of revision and the speed required. Rush work and work across many markets cost more, because they take more hands. We quote from a written brief, so the price reflects the actual work, and we state what is included — source files, formats, revision rounds — before we start.
