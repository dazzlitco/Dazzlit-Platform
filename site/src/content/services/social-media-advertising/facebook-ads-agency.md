## Facebook Ads Agency: what you get

Facebook advertising works when three things line up: the right audience, an ad worth stopping for, and a page that turns the click into an enquiry. Our job as your Facebook ads agency is to run all three as one system, in Meta Ads Manager, and to keep improving it against the numbers that matter — leads, sales and cost per acquisition — not vanity metrics.

Here is what the service covers.

- **Strategy and account structure.** We plan campaigns around your objectives — traffic, leads, sales or app installs — and build the account so each campaign has a clear job. Budgets sit where the return is, not spread thin across everything at once.
- **Audiences.** We build custom audiences from your customer lists, website visitors and engagers, and lookalike audiences that find people who behave like your best customers. We also test interest and broad targeting, so the account is not dependent on a single audience.
- **Creative.** We plan and produce the ad formats your audience responds to — single image, video, carousel and collection ads — and test new angles every month. Creative testing is continuous, not a one-off launch task, because the ad itself is usually the biggest lever on performance.
- **Tracking.** We set up the Meta Pixel and the Conversions API so conversions are reported accurately even when browsers block cookies. Without reliable tracking, optimisation is guesswork.
- **Optimisation.** We watch frequency so the same people do not see the same ad too often, manage budgets between prospecting and retargeting, and use the attribution window that matches how your customers actually buy.

Campaign objectives decide what the platform optimises for, so choosing them well matters. A campaign built for traffic will find clickers; a campaign built for leads or sales will find people more likely to convert. We match the objective to the outcome you need, then pick the ad formats that fit that job — short video for attention, carousel for ranges, single image for a clear offer. If you want to see what good looks like before you commit, our [Facebook ad examples](/resources/examples/facebook-ad-examples/) show the formats and angles we use most. And if you are new to the channel, our guide to [Facebook advertising](/resources/guides/facebook-advertising/) explains the basics, while [how Facebook ads work](/blog/how-do-facebook-ads-work/) covers the auction and the delivery system in plain language.

## Our Facebook Ads Agency process

Every engagement starts with an audit of what you already have: the account structure, the tracking, the audiences, the creative and the landing pages. We look for what is wasting spend and what is worth keeping, then agree the targets with you — the cost per lead or the return on ad spend the account has to hit for the work to pay.

From there, the work follows a fixed rhythm.

**1. Strategy.** We agree the audiences, the offers and the creative angles for the first month, and set the budget split between prospecting and retargeting. Prospecting finds new people; retargeting brings back the people who showed interest but did not convert.

**2. Build.** We set up the campaigns in Meta Ads Manager, build the custom and lookalike audiences, and install or check the Pixel and Conversions API. Every ad is checked against the landing page it points to, so the message and the destination agree.

**3. Launch and learn.** The first weeks are for testing: audiences against each other, creative angles against each other, and offers against each other. We keep the tests small enough to read clearly and big enough to learn from, and we do not judge a test on a handful of clicks.

**4. Optimise.** We move budget to the winners, pause the losers, and refresh creative before frequency climbs and performance fades. The attribution window is set to match your sales cycle, so a lead that converts a week after clicking still counts toward the campaign that found it.

**5. Scale and report.** When a campaign beats its target consistently, we scale it carefully — more budget, new audiences, new creative angles — and report what changed and why. If you want to run the day-to-day yourself one day, our guide on [how to run Facebook ads](/blog/how-to-run-facebook-ads/) covers the same process in a form your team can follow.

## Deliverables & reporting

You always know what you are paying for. Each month you receive a written report and a call to walk through it, covering spend, leads, cost per lead, CPM, CPA and ROAS, broken down by campaign, audience and creative. The report also records what we tested that month, what we learned, and what we are changing next — so the thinking is visible, not just the numbers.

Deliverables in a typical month include new or refreshed ad creative, new audience tests, landing page recommendations where the page is holding the campaign back, and the monthly report itself. If you want to track the same numbers yourself between reports, our [Facebook ads report template](/resources/templates/facebook-ads-report-template/) gives you the structure we use. And because cost is the question almost every new client asks first, our article on [Facebook ads cost](/blog/facebook-ads-cost/) explains what drives it — audience competition, creative quality, landing page conversion and budget — before you spend anything.

## Results / case study

Results depend on the offer, the audience and the budget, so we do not promise a fixed number. What we can show is what paid advertising has done for a real client: for a home services company, Dazzlit's paid advertising generated 450+ qualified leads and cut cost per lead by 38%. You can read the full story on our [case studies](/case-studies/) page.

The pattern behind that result is the same one we apply to every account: a clear target, clean tracking, steady creative testing and budget moved toward what works. Facebook rewards accounts that learn quickly. The first month is usually the most expensive per lead, because the account is still testing; as winning audiences and creative emerge, cost per lead normally falls. That is why we judge a campaign over weeks, not days, and why we report on leads and revenue rather than clicks and impressions.

## Pricing & engagement models

Paid social has two costs, and it helps to keep them separate. The media spend goes to Meta and buys the impressions; the management fee pays for the strategy, the creative, the build and the optimisation. Both are scoped in your proposal, so there are no surprises after launch.

What affects the price is the scope of the work: how many campaigns and audiences you need, how much creative has to be produced each month, how many markets you advertise in, and how much testing the account needs. A single-market lead generation account costs less to run than a multi-market ecommerce account with weekly creative refreshes, and we price accordingly.

We work in two main ways. Most clients are on a monthly retainer, which covers the ongoing management, testing and reporting described above. Shorter fixed-scope projects are available for a defined job, such as an account audit, a launch, or a creative sprint. Either way, the proposal states the scope, the fee and the media budget before work starts.
